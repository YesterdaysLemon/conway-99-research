## Finite-field frame identities

Greaves, Iverson, Jasper, and Mixon prove in Proposition 3.5 of
[Frames over finite fields: Basic theory and equiangular lines in unitary
geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12977) that a spanning vector frame is
`c`-tight exactly when its Gramian satisfies `G^2=cG`. Remark 3.7 retains the
spanning requirement.

This supports the square-zero centered Gram identity when `c=0` and spanning
is separately checked. It does not supply a joint law for 99 rank-six
projectors or for the cyclic word `tr(P_x P_y P_z P_y)`.

Status: `CITED`; transfer to triple projector moments: `UNKNOWN`.

## Tight fusion frames and cubatures

Bachoc and Ehler's [Tight p-fusion
frames](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1798) works with real Euclidean
Grassmannians, positive weights, normalized invariant measure, and positive
fusion-frame potentials. Theorem 5.3 identifies tight `p`-fusion frames with
exact integration on a specified polynomial space. Definition 5.5 and Theorem
5.7 impose strength-`2p` cubature exactness, and Remark 5.8 notes that this is
strictly stronger for `p>=2`.

The frozen identity `sum_x P_x=0` over `F_3` is neither a positive weighted
real cubature nor proof of a `p=2` design. Even a single-index projector moment
formula would require an additional argument to identify the labeled joint
tensor involving three centers.

Status: `CITED`; direct transfer: `UNKNOWN`.

## Complex Grassmannian designs and association schemes

Roy's [Bounds for codes and designs in complex
subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.2317) obtains association schemes under
a conjunction of hypotheses. Theorem 10.5 assumes a `(2t-2)`-design, an
`A`-code with exactly `t` inner-product values, and the required annihilator
polynomials. The subsequent `t=2` example still assumes a 2-design and exactly
two trace values.

The modular star spaces have not been shown to form such a design, angle-class
code, or annihilator system. Equality of the full labeled `g` and `H` matrices
therefore does not invoke Roy's conclusion. The exact hostile pair in this
package has identical full `g` and `H` but different `tau`, under its explicitly
weaker premises.

Status: `CITED`; association-scheme closure here: `UNKNOWN`.

## Triple regularity

Suda's [Coherent configurations and triply regular association schemes
obtained from spherical designs](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.5169) defines
triple regularity in Definition 2.7: the number of points in three prescribed
relations to a labeled triple depends only on the six pair-relation indices.
Lemma 2.8 relates this to coherent configurations on every basepoint
subconstituent. Corollary 3.1 derives triple regularity for tight real
spherical 4-designs and for a separately constrained spherical 3-design case.

Those design and dual-parameter hypotheses are extra. Strong regularity by
itself is not the cited tight-design premise, and no result in the source
identifies the modular trace `tau` with a scheme triple-intersection number.

Status: `CITED`; generic three-center closure: `UNKNOWN`.

## Terwilliger or subconstituent modules

Tomiyama and Yamazaki's [The Subconstituent Algebra of a Strongly Regular
Graph](https://doi.org/10.2206/kyushujm.48.323) studies the basepoint algebra
of an actual strongly regular graph. A modern graph-level definition is also
stated by Hanaki and Yoshikawa in [Terwilliger algebras and some related
algebras defined by finite connected simple
graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07109): the algebra is generated by the
actual adjacency matrix and the idempotents of the fixed-vertex distance
partition.

Thus a Terwilliger module is not generated by the parameter tuple
`(99,14,1,2)` alone. The actual basepoint adjacency actions are input. The
shared-incidence control here is disconnected and not strongly regular, so
its modules cannot be substituted for the missing target graph.

Status: `CITED`; parameter-only module closure: `UNKNOWN`.

## Linear triple incidence

The applicable statement at the current premise level is elementary and
checked directly. For a binary linear triple incidence `B` with row degree 7
and column degree 3, its point graph satisfies

```text
BB^T=A+7I over Z,
BB^T=A+I  over F_3.
```

This gives a 14-regular point graph. It does not give connectedness,
`lambda=1`, `mu=2`, triple regularity, or a projector-moment closure. The
control is an exact counterexample to those unstated implications: it has
component sizes `27,36,36`, nonuniform common-neighbor counts, and 1,098
extra graph triangles.

Status: `DERIVED`; stronger closure from these premises: `REFUTED`.

## Fixed-y Gram rank

For fixed `y`, put

```text
Q_x=P_y P_x P_y.
```

Each `Q_x` is a self-adjoint endomorphism of the nondegenerate six-space
`E_y`. The self-adjoint endomorphisms of a six-space form a 21-dimensional
space, and

```text
tau_y[x,z]=tr(Q_x Q_z)
```

is their trace Gram matrix. Hence

```text
rank_F3(tau_y)<=21.
```

Also `sum_x Q_x=0`, the `y` row is `g_y*`, and the diagonal is `H_y*`.
The two 99-projector controls in this package attain rank 21 exactly while
having different diagonal and entry distributions. They repeat 33 projector
types three times and have no coupled 231-column incidence, so they establish
sharpness only for the stated projector-level bound.

Status: `DERIVED`; target-level rank or distribution consequence: `UNKNOWN`.

## Audit verdict

The literature routes require premises not yet available:

- finite-field vector-frame theory gives `D^2=0`, not triple projector moments;
- real fusion-frame identities require positivity and proved cubature
  exactness;
- Grassmannian association schemes require design, angle-class, and
  annihilator hypotheses;
- triple regularity requires an actual scheme plus stronger design or
  regularity assumptions; and
- Terwilliger modules require the actual basepoint adjacency action.

The next useful object must couple different fixed-center Gram slices. A
graph-typed joint law for the family `{tau_y}`, or an equivalent
four-center/Terwilliger-localizer invariant, is still missing. Conway-99,
rank 11, and `n3=4158` remain `UNKNOWN`; `Q>=7060` remains `NOT PROVED`.
