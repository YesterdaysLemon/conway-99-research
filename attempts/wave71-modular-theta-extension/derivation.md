# Level-7 neighbor, mod-7 theta reduction, and forced short vectors

Claim label: `DERIVED` (discovery; independent verification required).

Everything is conditional on the Wave 66 package and a hypothetical
\(\operatorname{srg}(99,14,1,2)\).  Write \(A\) for its adjacency matrix and
\(q\in\{2,4,\ldots,16\}\) for the 7-primary length of the Wave 66
discriminant group.

## 1. The 99 vectors mark the \(\mathbf Z/9\) factor

Wave 66 gives
\[
C_{ij}=\langle u_i,u_j\rangle
=3\delta_{ij}-A_{ij}+\frac19,\qquad
M=\langle u_i-u_j\rangle_{\mathbf Z}.
\]
For every \(i,j,k\), \(C_{ij}-C_{ik}\) is integral, so \(u_i\in M^*\).
All \(u_i\) have the same class
\[
\alpha=[u_i]\in M^*/M.
\]
Its finite quadratic and bilinear values are
\[
q_M(\alpha)=\frac{\langle u_i,u_i\rangle}{2}
=\frac{14}{9}=\frac59\pmod{\mathbf Z},\qquad
b_M(\alpha,\alpha)=\frac{28}{9}=\frac19\pmod{\mathbf Z}.
\]
The latter has denominator nine, so \(\alpha\) has order nine.  Because the
3-primary part of \(M^*/M\) is cyclic of order nine, \(\alpha\) generates
it.

The order-three subgroup
\[
H=\langle3\alpha\rangle
\]
is isotropic, since \(q_M(3\alpha)=9q_M(\alpha)=14=0\pmod{\mathbf Z}\).
Moreover \(H^\perp=H\) inside the \(\mathbf Z/9\) summand: if \(x\alpha\)
is orthogonal to \(3\alpha\), then
\(b_M(x\alpha,3\alpha)=x/3=0\pmod{\mathbf Z}\), hence \(3\mid x\).

## 2. Exact even neighbor and its scaled dual

The standard isotropic-overlattice correspondence therefore gives
\[
L=M_H=M+\mathbf Z(3u_0).
\]
It is an even positive-definite lattice of rank 44, \([L:M]=3\), and
\[
L^*/L\cong H^\perp/H\oplus(\mathbf Z/7)^q
\cong(\mathbf Z/7)^q,\qquad \det L=7^q.
\tag{1}
\]
This removes the 3-primary part rather than merely forgetting it.

The marked vectors \(v_i=3u_i\) all belong to \(L\), and retain exact
graph data:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle v_i,v_i\rangle&=28,\\
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle&=-8 &&(i\sim j),\\
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle&=1 &&(i\not\sim j),\\
\sum_i v_i&=0,\qquad
\sum_i v_iv_i^{\mathsf T}=63I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus a lattice satisfying (1) is not enough: it must also contain the marked
99-vector frame (2).

Now put
\[
K=\sqrt7\,L^*.
\]
Because \(L^*/L\) is elementary 7-primary, \(7L^*\subseteq L\).
Consequently \(7\langle x,y\rangle\in\mathbf Z\) for \(x,y\in L^*\).
The finite quadratic form has denominator seven, so
\(7\langle x,x\rangle\in2\mathbf Z\).  Hence \(K\) is even and integral.
Its exact invariants are
\[
\det K=\frac{7^{44}}{\det L}=7^{44-q},\qquad
K^*/K\cong(\mathbf Z/7)^{44-q}.
\tag{3}
\]
Both \(q\) and \(44-q\) are positive, so \(L\) and \(K\) have exact level
seven, not merely level dividing seven.

Since \(L^*\subseteq M^*\), the imported Wave 66 bound gives
\[
\boxed{\min K=7\min L^*\ge14.}
\tag{4}
\]

## 3. Theta normalization and Skoruppa reduction

Use
\[
\Theta_K(z)=\sum_{x\in K}e^{2\pi iz\langle x,x\rangle/2}
=1+\sum_{n\ge1}a_nq^n,\qquad
a_n=\#\{x\in K:\langle x,x\rangle=2n\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (4) says exactly
\[
a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_6=0.
\tag{6}
\]

Skoruppa's cited main theorem applies because \(K\) is even, integral, and
has prime-power level \(7\).  The Smith factors of a rank-44 lattice with
discriminant group \((\mathbf Z/7)^s\), \(s=44-q\), are
\[
1^{\,44-s},\quad7^{\,s}.
\]
Their sum is
\[
e(K)=44-s+7s=44+6s.
\]
Therefore there is an integral level-one modular form \(f\) such that
\[
\Theta_K\equiv f\pmod7,\qquad
f\in M_{e(K)/2}(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)),
\]
with exact weight
\[
w=\frac{e(K)}2=22+3(44-q)=154-3q.
\tag{7}
\]
The eight weights, in increasing \(q\), are
\[
148,142,136,130,124,118,112,106.
\]

Over \(\mathbf Z_{(7)}\), level-one forms are generated by \(E_4,E_6\).
At weight \(w\), `exact_check.py` uses every monomial
\[
E_4^aE_6^b,\qquad4a+6b=w.
\tag{8}
\]
The checker recomputes
\[
E_4=1+240\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_3(n)q^n,\qquad
E_6=1-504\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_5(n)q^n.

In particular \(E_6\equiv1\pmod7\) coefficientwise.  It then performs exact
Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbf F_7\) using coefficients only through

All eight rows admit the mandatory gap (6), so no row is deleted.  The exact
no-go boundary is:

| \(q\) | weight \(w\) | largest all-zero prefix still feasible mod 7 | next zero is inconsistent | forced bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,4,6,8,10,12,14 | 148,142,136,130,124,118,112 | \(a_1=\cdots=a_{13}=0\) | \(a_{14}=0\) | \(\min K\le28\) |

For \(q\le14\), the unique mod-7 solution with the thirteen-term gap has

a_9=2\pmod7.
\]
More usefully, retaining the two free short coefficients after only the

\[
\boxed{a_7+a_8+a_9\equiv2\pmod7.}
\tag{9}

Every \(a_n\) is even by \(x\leftrightarrow-x\), so
\[
\boxed{a_7+a_8+a_9\equiv2\pmod{14}.}
\tag{10}

Thus the \(q=16\) row forces at least one vector of \(K\) of norm 14, 16, or
18.  This is a forced short vector, not a contradiction.

## 4. Short vectors are exact integer \(-4\)-eigenvectors

For \(y\in M^*\), Wave 66 writes
\[
t_i=\langle y,u_i\rangle=n_i+\frac a{99},\qquad11\mid a,
\]
and proves
\[
\sum_i t_i=0,\qquad At=-4t,\qquad
7\langle y,y\rangle=\sum_i t_i^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Membership in \(L^*\) adds
\[
3t_i=\langle y,3u_i\rangle\in\mathbf Z.
\]
Hence \(a\equiv0,33,\) or \(66\pmod{99}\).  In the two nonintegral cases,
the exact unconstrained energy bound is
\[
\sum_i t_i^2\ge\frac{33(99-33)}{99}=22.
\tag{12}
\]
Therefore a vector \(y\in L^*\) with
\(\langle y,y\rangle\le18/7\) must have \(a=0\).  Then \(t\) is integral.

Conversely, if
\[
t\in\mathbf Z^{99},\qquad
\sum_i t_i=0,\qquad At=-4t,
\tag{13}
\]
then \(y=\frac17\sum_i t_i u_i\) has
\(\langle y,u_j-u_k\rangle=t_j-t_k\in\mathbf Z\) and
\(\langle y,3u_j\rangle=3t_j\in\mathbf Z\), so \(y\in L^*\).
Equations (11)--(13) give a norm-preserving bijection
\[
\boxed{
\{z\in K:\|z\|^2=n\}
\longleftrightarrow
\{t\in\mathbf Z^{99}:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}t=0,\ At=-4t,\
\sum_i t_i^2=n\}
}
\tag{14}
\]
for \(n\le18\).

Consequently, in the \(q=16\) row, if \(N_n\) denotes the number of integer

\boxed{N_{14}+N_{16}+N_{18}\equiv2\pmod{14}.}
\tag{15}

## 6. Signed-support structure

Let \(P\) and \(N\) be the positive and negative unit supports, each of size
\(s\), and let \(h_P,h_N\) count same-sign induced edges.  Summing
\(At=-4t\) over either side gives
\[
e(P,N)=2h_P+4s=2h_N+4s,
\]
so \(h_P=h_N=h\), and the total induced edge count is
\[
e(P\cup N)=4s+4h.
\tag{16}
\]
For a vertex subset of size \(m\), the positive restricted eigenvalue gives
the exact edge bound
\[
e\le\frac{3m}{2}+\frac{11m^2}{198}.
\tag{17}
\]

### Norm 14

For \(m=14\), (17) is \(287/9\), while (16) is \(28+4h\).
Thus \(h=0\): the support is 4-regular bipartite on \(7+7\).

Pairs on one side have
\[
7\binom42=42
\]
common-neighbor incidences through the other side.  There are 21 pairs and
each has at most \(\mu=2\) common neighbors, so equality holds everywhere.
The support is the symmetric \(2\)-\((7,4,2)\) design, equivalently the
incidence graph of complements of Fano-plane lines.

There is also a forced external design.  Every same-side pair already uses
both common neighbors inside the support, so an outside vertex meets at most
one point of each side.  Its eigenvector equation says it meets equally many
on both sides.  Exactly 70 outside vertices meet one point on each side;
the remaining 15 meet none.  Call these sets \(X\) and \(Y\).

For \(y\in Y\), each of the seven positive support vertices has exactly two
common neighbors with \(y\), in disjoint fibers of \(X\).  These 14 vertices
exhaust the degree of \(y\), so \(Y\) is independent.  Two vertices of \(Y\)
therefore have exactly two common neighbors in \(X\).
If \(c_x=|N(x)\cap Y|\), then
\[
\sum_{x\in X}c_x=15\cdot14=210,\qquad
\sum_{x\in X}\binom{c_x}{2}=2\binom{15}{2}=210.
\]
Hence \(\sum c_x^2=630\).  Cauchy's inequality gives the same lower bound
\(210^2/70=630\), so equality forces \(c_x=3\) for every \(x\).
Thus the 70 neighborhoods in \(Y\) form a
\[
\boxed{2\text{-}(15,3,2)\text{ design}.}
\tag{18}
\]

### Norm 16

For \(m=16\), (17) is \(344/9\), so (16) leaves \(h=0\) or \(1\).
If \(h=1\), the opposite-side degrees are two 5's and six 4's.  They produce
\[
2\binom52+6\binom42=56
\]
common-neighbor incidences among pairs on one side.  But that side has one
adjacent pair, allowing \(\lambda=1\), and 27 nonadjacent pairs, allowing
\(\mu=2\), for total capacity \(1+54=55\).  Contradiction.

Thus a norm-16 solution forces
\[
\boxed{\text{a 4-regular bipartite induced graph on }8+8.}
\tag{19}
\]

### Norm 18

First exclude the last mixed profile: six \(+1\)'s, eight \(-1\)'s, and one
\(+2\).  At the \(+2\) coordinate, the equation forces adjacency to all
eight negatives and no positives.  If \(e_P\) and \(e_N\) are the unit-side
edge counts, summing their equations gives
\[
e_N=e_P+4,\qquad e(P,N)=2e_P+24.
\]
Including the eight edges from the \(+2\) coordinate, the 15-vertex induced
subgraph has \(4e_P+36\ge36\) edges.  Formula (17) at \(m=15\) is exactly
35, a contradiction.

For the remaining \(9+9\) unit profile, (17) is 45 and (16) leaves
\[
h\in\{0,1,2\}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(h=2\) and the two same-sign edges on one side share an endpoint, the
opposite-side degrees give
\[
\binom62+2\binom52+6\binom42=71
\]
common-neighbor incidences.  The two adjacent pairs and 34 nonadjacent pairs
have capacity \(2\lambda+34\mu=70\), impossible.  Hence for \(h=2\), the
two same-sign edges on **each** side are disjoint.  In that case the count
equals 70 exactly, forcing saturation and no outside common neighbor for
any same-side pair.

## 7. Exact boundary

No one of (18)--(20) is excluded.  The result is therefore not a proof of
nonexistence.  It is an exact shift of the hardest \(q=16\) row into three
finite signed-support structures, with the count congruence (15).

The lower-level scalar lattice conditions remain separate from existence of
the marked frame (2).  Conway-99 and novelty remain `UNKNOWN`.
