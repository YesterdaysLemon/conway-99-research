# Derivation

## 1. From a block dependence to a line-sum coloring

Assume the prism-free endpoint `P=0`.  Retain the notation of Waves 171 and
172: `B` is point--triangle incidence, `A` is the point adjacency matrix, and
`D=C+J` is the symmetric centered ternary Gram matrix on the 231 triangle
blocks.

Over `F_3`,

```text
K=B^T*B,
K^2=B^T*(A+I)*B,
D=2(K-K^2)=B^T*A*B.                               (1)
```

Wave 172 proves that a weight-three word in `ker(D)` must, after scaling,
have the form

```text
D_i+D_j+D_k=0,                                    (2)
```

where the three blocks are pairwise disjoint, have no cross edges, and
therefore induce `3K3`.  Let `U` be their nine points and let `q=1_U`.
Because `B(e_i+e_j+e_k)=q`, (1)--(2) give

```text
B^T*A*q=0 mod 3.                                  (3)
```

Put `h=Aq` over the integers.  A point of `U` has `h=2`.  An outside point
has at most one neighbor in each of the three triangles, hence
`h in {0,1,2,3}`.

Let `c` count outside points with `h=3`.  Counting boundary edges and pairs
of boundary edges gives

```text
n0+n1+n2+n3=90,
n1+2n2+3n3=108,
n2+3n3=54.
```

Thus the outside multiplicities are

```text
(n0,n1,n2,n3)=(36-c,3c,54-3c,c).                 (4)
```

Reduce `h` modulo three.  Equation (3) says that its three colors sum to
zero on every triangle block.  Every block is consequently of type

```text
000, 111, 222, or 012.                            (5)
```

The global color-class sizes are

```text
(36,3c,63-3c).
```

If `T012=3t`, point--block incidence gives

```text
T000=84-t,
T111=7c-t,
T222=147-7c-t.                                    (6)
```

## 2. Five cells and the cross-color equation

Refine the colors into five cells

```text
U, Z0={outside h=0}, O1={h=1}, T2={h=2}, Z3={h=3},
```

of sizes

```text
9, 36-c, 3c, 54-3c, c.                           (7)
```

The SRG identity

```text
A^2=12I-A+2J
```

determines, for a point with neighbor count `h=r`, the sum of the neighbor
counts around it:

```text
sum_(y~x) h(y)=28  for x in U,
sum_(y~x) h(y)=18-r otherwise.                    (8)
```

Combining degrees, (8), the fixed `U` boundary, and the fact that every
rainbow triangle contributes one neighbor of each other color gives the
following undirected edge-count matrix:

```text
             U            Z0             O1             T2             Z3
U            9             0             3c         108-6c             3c
Z0           0       108-c-t        3t-3c         216-12c        72+3c-t
O1          3c         3t-3c        21c-3t          3t-3c             3c
T2      108-6c        216-12c        3t-3c     324-15c-3t     9c+3t-216
Z3          3c        72+3c-t          3c       9c+3t-216       72-2c-t
```

At each individual color-zero point, its numbers of color-one and
color-two neighbors are equal: every incident nonmonochromatic triangle is
rainbow.  Applying this separately to `Z0` or `Z3` forces

```text
t=72-3c.                                          (9)
```

Nonnegativity of (6)--(7) and the edge matrix leaves

```text
8<=c<=18.                                         (10)
```

## 3. Interlacing leaves only c=8,9

The five cell indicators span a quotient test space.  The three-dimensional
Krylov space generated by `1_U` is invariant under `A` and carries the SRG
eigenvalues `14,3,-4`.  On its two-dimensional orthogonal complement,
interlacing requires `3I-A` to be positive semidefinite.

Use the two cell-constant vectors

```text
v1=(0,3c(18-c),-2(36-c)(18-c),c(36-c),0),
v2=(0,0,18-c,-2c,3(18-c)).
```

Both are orthogonal to `1`, `1_U`, and `A1_U`.  Exact substitution of (9)
in the edge matrix gives a `2 by 2` Gram matrix `G`.  Its second diagonal
entry and determinant factor as

```text
G22  = 648(c-18)(c-12),
detG = -3359232*c(c-12)(c-9)(c-18)^3.             (11)
```

For `c=10,11`, the determinant is negative.  At `c=12`, the first diagonal
entry is negative, and for `13<=c<=17`, `G22` is negative.

At `c=18`, the `T2` cell is empty and the displayed basis degenerates.  Use
instead the cell-constant vector

```text
v=(0,2,-1,0,1)
```

on `(U,Z0,O1,T2,Z3)`.  It is orthogonal to `1`.  The exact edge matrix gives

```text
v^T v=144,
v^T A v=1584,
v^T(3I-A)v=3*144-1584=-1152,
```

again contradicting interlacing.  Hence

```text
c in {8,9}.                                       (12)
```

This is an exact quotient/interlacing argument; no graph enumeration is
used.

## 4. Common-neighbor convexity eliminates c=8

For any point set `S` in this SRG, write `d_S(x)=|N(x) intersect S|`.
Counting a common neighbor for every pair in `S` gives

```text
sum_x binomial(d_S(x),2)
 = 2*binomial(|S|,2)-e(S),                        (13)
```

because adjacent pairs have one common neighbor and nonadjacent pairs have
two.

Take `S=Z3`.  At `(c,t)=(8,48)`, the edge matrix gives

```text
|Z3|=8, e(Z3)=8,
sum_(x in U,Z0,O1,T2,Z3) d_Z3(x)=(24,48,24,0,16).
```

For fixed integral degree sum, convexity minimizes the sum of
`binomial(d,2)` by balancing the degrees.  The five cellwise minima are

```text
21+20+0+0+8=49.
```

Equation (13), however, requires

```text
2*binomial(8,2)-8=48.
```

Contradiction.  Therefore

```text
c=9, t=45.                                        (14)
```

## 5. Equality forces a forbidden prism

At (14), the color sizes are `(36,27,36)`.  Equation (13) is tight for
`S=Z3`: both sides equal 63.  Equality in every cellwise convexity bound
forces the pointwise `Z3`-degrees

```text
cell:       U  Z0  O1  T2  Z3
degree:     3   2   1   0   2.                   (15)
```

In particular, `G[Z3]` is 2-regular.  Label each `z in Z3` by its unique
neighbor in each of the three original `U` triangles.  Consider consecutive
vertices `z_-,z,z_+` on a cycle of `G[Z3]`.

For one original triangle, let the label of `z` be `u`.

1. Neither neighbor of `z` can also have label `u`: otherwise the adjacent
   pair in `Z3` would have a color-two common neighbor, whereas its unique
   common neighbor lies on its monochromatic color-zero triangle.
2. The two neighbors cannot share either alternative label `u'`.  If they
   did, then the nonadjacent pair `(z,u')` would have three common
   neighbors: the selected label `u` inside the original triangle and the
   two cycle neighbors.  This contradicts `mu=2`.

Thus, in every original triangle, the labels of `z_-,z,z_+` are all
different.  Over three symbols the label sequence has period three.  A
cycle of length six or nine would therefore contain two distinct vertices
with the same three `U` neighbors, contradicting `lambda=1, mu=2` because
those vertices would have at least three common neighbors.  Hence

```text
G[Z3]=3K3.                                         (16)
```

Every component in (16) is a triangle block.  Along such a component, its
three labels in each original `U` triangle are all distinct.  Therefore
each `Z3` triangle block is joined to each original triangle block by a
perfect matching.  Those six points induce a triangular prism.

This contradicts the endpoint hypothesis `P=0`.  The assumed word (2)
cannot exist.

## Conclusion and boundary

Wave 172 already gives dual distances one and two equal to zero and
classifies every possible weight-three word.  The argument above eliminates
the remaining equal-coefficient type.  Therefore the endpoint centered
code satisfies

```text
B1=B2=B3=0,
d(W^perp)>=4.                                      (17)
```

Equivalently, the 231 projective columns of any generator matrix for `W`
form a cap over `F_3`.

This is a strict strengthening of the endpoint code package, but it does
not by itself exclude the endpoint, determine its ternary rank, improve
`n3<=4158`, construct a graph, or resolve Conway-99.
