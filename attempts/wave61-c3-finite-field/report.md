## Result

No finite-field obstruction was found.  In particular, none of the 1,140
component triples is eliminated, even by the strongest test here: the exact
span test over `F_2` for all 630 prescribed row-pair parities generated by
every fibre-compatible positive six-set.

This is a rigorous negative result about a bounded relaxation, not evidence
that an incidence design exists.  The conditional lane and the Conway-99
endpoint both remain `UNKNOWN`; novelty is also `UNKNOWN`.

## Universal binary consequences

Let `F_0,F_1,F_2` be the indicators of the three twelve-point fibres and let
`C_0,C_1,C_2` be the indicators of the three twelve-point components.  Every
hypothetical column of `B` meets each fibre and each component twice.
Therefore all six indicators lie in `ker(B^T)` over `F_2`.  They have exactly
one linear relation,

```text
F_0 + F_1 + F_2 = C_0 + C_1 + C_2 = all-ones,
```

so their span has dimension five.  Consequently `rank_F2(B) <= 31`.
Moreover, every row of `B` has weight ten, so `G=B B^T` has zero diagonal
over `F_2`: it is alternating and has even rank at most 30.

The complete independently reconstructed rank census is:

| `rank_F2(G)` | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| triples | 67 | 415 | 412 | 185 | 51 | 10 |

Thus the rank ceiling has at least six dimensions of slack in every lane.

## Quadratic and Witt boundary

The even-weight space

```text
E_60 = {v in F_2^60 : wt(v) is even}
```

has radical spanned by the all-ones vector.  Its 58-dimensional quotient
carries the quadratic form `q(v)=wt(v)/2 mod 2` of minus type (Arf invariant
one), since its quadratic Gauss sum is `-2^30`.

For a row-selection vector `x`, the target Gram matrix determines

```text
q(B^T x) = |x| + sum_{i<j} G_ij x_i x_j  (mod 2).
```

The radical of `G` sometimes contains a vector on which this quadratic form
is one, but its `q=0` subspace always contains the five known partition
indicators.  After quotienting by those five required kernel vectors, the
dimension/Witt embedding screen has slack between 10 and 20 dimensions for
all 1,140 targets.  Hence neither the alternating form nor this quadratic
refinement supplies an obstruction.

Over the odd primes `3,5,7,11`, exact congruence reduction records rank,
discriminant square class, and anisotropic dimension.  All ranks are at most
32.  Since a nondegenerate complement of dimension at least 28 can absorb
either finite-field discriminant class, no odd-prime Witt obstruction occurs.
At `p=7` every target has rank 32; the other complete histograms are retained
in `exact-results.json`.

These statements concern unrestricted finite-field Gram factors.  Such a
factor need not have 60 distinct binary columns, each with the required
six-set profile.

## The 21 component/fibre patterns

A column has a `3 x 3` table of component-by-fibre cell counts, with every
row sum and column sum equal to two.  There are exactly 21 integral tables:

- 6 doubled permutation matrices, reducing to binary weight zero;
- 9 tables reducing to a four-cycle;
- 6 complements of permutation matrices, reducing to a six-cycle.

Their binary images are all 16 matrices with even row and column sums, a
four-dimensional cycle space.  The induced parity-moment system has rank 11,
and every one of the 1,140 targets is consistent with it.

## Full fixed-pair coupling

For each component, its 66 local vertex pairs have prescribed nonnegative
counts summing to 60.  A candidate column chooses one positive-count pair
from each component, and the three pair profiles must sum to `(2,2,2)` over
the fibres.  This gives between 15,936 and 27,200 distinct candidate six-sets

## Cubic moment boundary

Let `T_ijk` count columns containing three distinct rows `i,j,k`.  Every
column containing a fixed pair `{i,j}` has four further rows, so any design
must satisfy

```text
sum_{k not in {i,j}} T_ijk = 4 G_ij.
```

The 1,140 targets pass all consequences determined by `G`: each row lies in
100 triple incidences and the total third-order incidence is 1,200.  The pair
margins take seven histogram types, all recorded exactly.

This identifies the missing invariant precisely.  The Gram target fixes the
pair margins of `T`, but not a nonnegative integral tensor `T`, and still less
a tensor realized by the same 60 distinct six-subsets.  Degree-three and
higher compatibility is where finite-field Gram analysis stops.

## Countercontrols

The checker retains small exact examples preventing three tempting
overclaims:

- independent even rows can have zero Gram matrix, so
  `rank(B B^T)=rank(B)` is false in characteristic two;
- a hyperbolic alternating block has an even-row Gram factor, so
  alternating singularity alone is not an obstruction;
- the six partition indicators are not independent; their span has
  dimension five.

## Recommended change of space

The next serious lane should keep integer multiplicity and third-order
realizability instead of trying more field ranks:

1. Build the nonnegative integer cone generated by the 15,936--27,200 allowed
   six-set columns for each component triple.  First solve the rational cone
   problem and retain an exact Farkas dual certificate for any exclusion.
2. If the rational cone is feasible, test semigroup membership with exact
   integer counts and distinct-column upper bounds `x_s <= 1`.  This directly
   attacks the invariant lost by Wave 61.
3. In parallel, project generators to carefully chosen triple coordinates
   and search for sparse dual inequalities.  A sparse violated inequality
   would be much easier to verify than a monolithic SAT nonhit.
4. A second algebraic route is the complete weight enumerator of the binary
   row code of `B`: MacWilliams/Krawtchouk moment constraints retain more than
   the Gram matrix while remaining suitable for exact linear programming.

The 630-bit parity filter remains a cheap certified prefilter for any expanded
component census, although it removes no lane in the present 1,140 cases.
