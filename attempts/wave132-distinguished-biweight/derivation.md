## 3. Split enumerator identities

For `x in C` of weight `w`, define

```text
P_wj = #{(x,u): wt(x)=w, |supp(x) intersect supp(r_u)|=j}.
```

Since every coordinate occurs in 14 neighborhood rows,

```text
sum_j P_wj = 99 A_w,
sum_j j P_wj = 14 w A_w.                            (1)
```

Also

```text
<r_u,x> = (Ax)_u = x_u,
```

because `Ax=x` on `C`.  Exactly `w` roots therefore give odd
intersection:

```text
sum_(j odd) P_wj = w A_w.                            (2)
```

The analogous dual/closed variables obey

```text
sum_j Q_wj = 99 B_w,
sum_j j Q_wj = 15 w B_w,
sum_(j odd) Q_wj = w B_w,                            (3)
```

because `(I+A)y=y` on `D`.

For mixed systems, `C` and `D` are orthogonal, so every intersection is
even.  The first moments are respectively `15w A_w` for image words
against closed rows and `14w B_w` for dual words against neighborhood
rows.  Every split row also obeys the set-theoretic range

```text
max(0,w+m-99) <= j <= min(w,m),
```

where `m=14` or 15.
