# Wave 66 failed routes and exact boundary

## Tensor-power rank alone

The entrywise powers of either two-distance Gram matrix have the form
\[
(1-\beta^m)I+(\alpha^m-\beta^m)A+\beta^mJ.
\]
For the first few powers all relevant eigenspace ranks fit easily inside the
corresponding symmetric-tensor dimensions.  This gives no endpoint
contradiction.

## Equiangular-line counting bounds

The lift gives 99 real equiangular lines in dimension 45 with common angle
\(1/7\).  Gerzon's bound and the relative bound are far too large to exclude
99.  Neumann's odd-reciprocal integrality condition is satisfied because
\(7\) is odd.

## Bare Smith compatibility

The determinant rows
\[
\det M=9\cdot7^{44-r},\qquad28\le r\le44,
\]
all satisfy the elementary exponent and rank conditions.  A discriminant
group is not a lattice construction, and a lattice construction would not by
itself be a graph.

## Geometry-of-numbers bound

The bound \(\min(M^*)\ge2\), combined with Blichfeldt's upper bound for the
44-dimensional Hermite constant, yields only \(r\ge18\).  The \(r=18\) row
passes that inequality.  This does not improve the already verified lower
bound \(r\ge28\), so no endpoint claim is made from it.

## Theta-series shortcut

Milgram's formula forces \(44-r\) to be positive and even, hence \(r\) to be
even and at most 42.  For every positive even value of \(44-r\), however, one
of the two determinant signs of the 7-primary quadratic form supplies the
required phase.  The exact
discriminant group still does not determine its quadratic form, genus, theta
series, or existence of the required 99-vector frame.  No full level-9 or
level-63 modular-form classification was completed.

## Remaining finite target

Equality in the dual-minimum bound can only arise from an integral
\(-4\)-eigenvector of squared norm 14.  Excluding those vectors would improve
the dual minimum, but it would still require a rigorous lattice or
geometry-of-numbers step to reach a graph contradiction.  Neither step is
claimed here.
