# Exact outside-block consequences and stopping wall

Claim label: `DERIVED` pending independent verification.

## 1. Frozen scope

For a Wave 210 representative, let `F` be its `14 x 85` support/outside
incidence matrix and let `A_S` be the fixed support graph.  A completion would
require a symmetric zero-diagonal `0/1` matrix `D` satisfying

```text
F D       = 2J - (A_S+I)F,                       (1)
D^2 + D   = 12I + 2J - F^T F,                   (2)
D 1       = 14 1 - F^T 1.                       (3)
```

The selected-union control also fixes all ten entries of `D` among its five
selected outside vertices.  No quotient of the 85 outside vertices and no
automorphism of a target graph is assumed.

For each of Wave 210's orbit representatives `0,4,29`, exact elimination gives

```text
rank_Q(F) = 13,       rank_Q([F;1^T]) = 14,
rank_F2(F) = 13,      rank_F2([F;1^T]) = 14.     (4)
```

Thus

```text
U = im(F^T) + <1>       has dimension 14,
K = ker(F) intersect 1^perp has dimension 71.    (5)
```

## 2. Forced action on `U`

Represent a vector of `U` as

```text
Phi(x,a) = F^T x + a 1,      x in Q^14, a in Q.
```

The kernel of `Phi` is generated by `(c,0)`, where
`c=(1^7,-1^7)` and `A_S c=3c`.  Equations (1) and (3), together with symmetry
of `D`, force

```text
D Phi(x,a)
 = Phi(-(A_S+I)x-a1, 2(1^T x)+14a).             (6)
```

The displayed 15-dimensional operator has `(c,0)` as a `-4` eigenvector.
Exact Faddeev-LeVerrier arithmetic, followed by quotienting this redundant
line, gives the characteristic polynomial of `D|U`:

```text
x^2 (x+2)^2 (x^2-2)^3
    (x^4 - 8x^3 - 50x^2 - 40x + 32).            (7)
```

In particular,

```text
tr(D|U)=4,                    tr((D|U)^2)=184.   (8)
```

This calculation is independent of which of the three representatives is
used: only `A_S`, the rank facts, and the frozen block equations enter it.

## 3. What the full quadratic block would force

For `v in K`, equations (1) and (3) show `Dv in K`.  Equation (2) would then
reduce to

```text
(D^2+D)v=12v,
```

so the symmetric operator `D|K` could have only eigenvalues `3` and `-4`.
Because a simple graph has `tr(D)=0`, (8) forces `tr(D|K)=-4`.  If the two
multiplicities are `m_3,m_-4`, then

```text
m_3+m_-4=71,
3m_3-4m_-4=-4,
```

and hence

```text
m_3=40,       m_-4=31.                           (9)
```

The square trace also matches exactly:

```text
184 + 40*9 + 31*16 = 1040 = sum_j (14-|F_j|).   (10)
```

Therefore trace, square trace, integrality, and the forced characteristic
polynomial do not contradict a completion.  Conditionally, the full outside
characteristic polynomial would be

```text
x^2 (x+2)^2 (x^2-2)^3
(x^4-8x^3-50x^2-40x+32)(x-3)^40(x+4)^31.        (11)
```

Modulo two, (2) reads `D^2+D=F^T F`; on `ker(F)` this becomes the square-free
idempotence relation `D^2+D=0`.  The ranks in (4) leave a 72-dimensional
`ker(F)` and a 71-dimensional `K` over `F_2`.  This is a restriction, but not
a parity contradiction.

## 4. A degree-type consequence

Every outside column has support size `t in {0,2,4}`.  Let `r` count how many
of the two support roots occur in the column, and let `n_t'` count neighbors
of that outside vertex whose support columns have size `t'`.  Summing (1)
over the fourteen support rows and using (3) gives

```text
n_0 - n_4 = t+r.                                  (12)
```

The committed orbit-29 witness satisfies (12) at all 85 vertices.  Its full
signature distribution is stored in `exact-results.json`.

## 5. Explicit hostile control and exact failure


## 6. Stopping wall

After (1) and (3), `D|U` is completely forced.  The unresolved content of (2)
is the choice of a symmetric `0/1` operator on the 71-dimensional complement
`K` with the entrywise common-neighbor constraints (13), not merely its
spectrum.  The present work neither constructs nor excludes that operator.

cap without a primal solution.  Two additional denominator-2 and denominator-4
integer-grid relaxation runs per orbit each reached 20-second caps without a
primal.  These are failed searches only; none is evidence of infeasibility.
