# Wave 124 failed routes and hostile controls

## Treating the K marking as primitive

`d_C` is primitive in `L`, but `b_C=sqrt(7)d_C` has exact divisibility
seven in `K`.  Ignoring this loses the sparse Fourier support and the
`n mod 7` sector split.

## Claiming that `r=28` always selects the C4 pattern

The implication uses the verified unit-only profiles.  For arbitrary
integer coordinates, a dot product of four can arise from other patterns.
The norm-20 profile theorem from Wave 96 is essential when `q^10` is used.

## Treating Fricke as fixed-index

Poisson summation couples K index 70 to L index 10.  A fixed-index
positivity involution is not available.

## Calling the full-level basis the level-seven basis

The constructed 18-dimensional space is
`J_22,10(SL2Z)`, embedded in `J_22,10(Gamma0(7))`.  The remaining
level-seven directions were not constructed.

## Using the tight-frame second moment as the upper bound

The second moment is exact but weak.  An explicit `A^2`-divisible
full-level cusp direction preserves scalar and second moments while moving
the old target coefficient.

## Declaring the modular cone unbounded

The oldform directions have signed coefficients and introduce Fourier
patterns such as `|ell|=6` on the norm-14 shell.  Those are forbidden by
the graph dictionary.  They show that modularity and moments alone are
insufficient, not that the positive graph-compatible cone is unbounded.

## Reusing the four-variable degree-32 interpolation

The alternating sum `ell_C` reduces exact target detection to an even
degree-eight polynomial and five moments.  The degree-32 four-coordinate
indicator remains correct but is unnecessarily large for this aggregate
question.
