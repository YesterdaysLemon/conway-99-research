# Retained failed routes and boundaries

## Total bijection over five candidates

The third block is defined only when an exact-three flag exists.
Fixed-center injectivity proves a bijection from existing flags to their
image, not surjectivity onto all five candidates. The genuine bijection
used in the capacity proof is the identity between the forward
third-block slots and reverse leaf-block slots, both equal to the same
five-set.

## Reverse existence

The source flag proves `j(S,T)=2`. It does not make `(y,S)` an
exact-three flag. In fact, if that matched reverse flag existed, the two
canonical relations would force a forbidden all-equal four-column word.

## Hidden column rescaling

The triangle columns are globally fixed. Both `c4` relations use those
same columns and normalize the leaf coefficient to one. Independently
rescaling columns for the two flags would change the frozen Gram and is
not allowed.

## Degree-five endpoint saturation

A pair degree of five means that the five possible simple triples through
the pair occur. It does not imply their five leaf values coincide.

## Low multiplicities

Patterns `(1,1)`, `(1,2)`, and `(2,2)` force respectively 8, 7, and 6
units of orientation deficit. They do not alone force a new circuit,
private label, or one-center fiber loss.

## Global scope

The combined capacity strengthens local structure but gives no endpoint
contradiction without an additional global row forcing both-oriented
labels. No absence statement is inferred from that missing row.
