## 2. Structural arithmetic and the repaired `m=27` proof

Independent enumeration reproduced the thirteen `q` profiles and all
reported `r<=19` eliminations.  The primitive projector

```text
E_-4=(27I-9A+J)/63
```

gave the same exact moment totals: `252` for `(m,e)=(29,90)` and `1386`
for `(29,89)`.  The last `m=29` branch would require integral outside
coordinates with sum `18` and square sum `14`, which is impossible.
For `m=30`, `z=5` is excluded by the zero projector total, and `z=4`
requires `25` against the exact lower bound `8+25=33`; hence `|Z|<=3`.

At `m=27`, equality forces the point profile `2^21 3^6` and a cubic
positive-meeting graph `J` on the six size-three points.  Exhaustive labeled
enumeration gives 70 graphs: 60 triangular prisms and 10 copies of `K3,3`.

The prism contradiction is direct.  The two triangular faces have common
labels `alpha` and `beta`.  On each of the three matching edges, deleting
its shared label leaves `alpha` and `beta` in the positive rectangle.  This
would provide three distinct original-edge witnesses for `alpha-beta`,
where an `L` edge has exactly two.

The submitted `K3,3` paragraph needed an explicit injectivity lemma, which
is now recorded rather than silently assumed.  Write the shared cross-edge
labels as `t_ij`.  Repetition on incident `K3,3` edges, combined with the
opposite positive rectangle, would force an `L` edge between two labels
already meeting in one indexed point.  Repetition on disjoint edges would
place one active label in four points.  Thus the nine `t_ij` are distinct.
Every different-row/different-column grid pair is then covered by exactly
the two opposite-corner rectangles.  A fixed grid label has four such
`L`-neighbors and exactly two remaining `L`-neighbors.  Its unique
size-two point has two distinct `R` neighbors, giving four remote endpoint
appearances.  Exact twofold witness multiplicity forces both neighbor
points to have the same two endpoint labels, contradicting point-family
linearity.

This proof uses two distinct facts that the short submission informally
merged: local zero-or-two row/column crossing shape creates a rectangle,
while the global definition of `L` supplies exactly two original-edge
witnesses.  With the injectivity lemma and this distinction explicit, both
`m=27` branches pass adversarial review without an automorphism assumption.

## 3. Residual-B semantic bridge

For `m=30`, all points have size two.  The audited semantics give:

```text
F: simple cubic triangle-free graph on 20 active labels
X=E(F): the 30 indexed point vertices
R: positive-crossing 2-factor on X
N A_R N^T = 2 A_L
D=A[X]=L(F) union R union Z
```

For each label `u`, the three incident `F` edges have two `R` neighbors
each.  Their six remote edges give a simple 2-regular graph on the six
`L`-neighbors of `u`.  Independent matching forbids repeated remote edges,
and triangle-freeness of `F` excludes `C3+C3`; the graph is therefore a
`C6`.  Its vertices are at `F`-distance at least three from `u`, and the
selections satisfy `v in L(u)` iff `u in L(v)`.  The independent solver
enumerated every such local domain and branched over every remaining
symmetric choice.

The codegree-two forcing rule was checked semantically.  If a non-`F`,
non-`L` label pair has two common `F` neighbors, those neighbors supply two
independent cross-edges.  The frozen crossing classification says that
leaving exactly those two would make the pair an `L` pair; because it is a
`K` pair, the unmatched third point-pair edge is forced into `Z`.  No
meeting-cross-edge assumption is used.

For the final two-Petersen model, the 165 enumerated eligible `Z` edges are
a safe complete superset: endpoints are distinct nonmeeting point vertices,
the edge is not already active, and all four endpoint-label pairs are in
`K`, not `L`.  Interactions that cannot coexist are rejected by recomputing
the exact Gram entries.  Thus no allowed `Z` edge is omitted, while any
over-admission only weakens the nonexistence test.

Finally, partitioning the SRG identity

```text
A^2=12I-A+2J,        A=[D C; C^T E]
```

gives the exact necessary matrix

```text
B=CC^T=12I-D-D^2+2J.
```

Every actual nonzero binary column `c` of `C` lies in `col(B)`, avoids every
pair with `B_ij=0`, and satisfies `Dc<=2-c`.  Complete positive-clique
enumeration plus exact nullspace orthogonality enumerates every binary vector
with these necessary properties.  Omitting zero columns, the 69-column
bound, and the outside graph `E` enlarges the feasible cone and is therefore
safe for an impossibility proof.  Exact coefficient inconsistency, a unique
solution with negative entries, or an exact Farkas separator is decisive.
