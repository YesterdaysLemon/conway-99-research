## 2. Lemma-by-lemma comparison

### Lemma 1: three-fibre fixed-point model

Agreement: `PASS_SCOPED`.

For an actual triangle `T={t0,t1,t2}`, each
`Xi=N(ti)\T` has 12 vertices. The `lambda=1` neighbourhood structure leaves
a perfect matching inside each fibre. For `x in Xi`, the nonadjacent pair
`(x,tj)` has exactly two common neighbours: `ti` and one vertex in `Xj`.
This gives mutually inverse cross-fibre bijections. After label transport, a
cross-fibre transversal closes exactly when its label is fixed by
`sigma_T=f20 f12 f01`.

Restriction made explicit by Stage 1: a permutation cannot move exactly one
point, so `q(T)=1` is impossible. The candidate does not use `q=1`, and its
`q=2` control respects this restriction.

### Lemma 2: relation degrees and endpoint counts

Agreement: `PASS`.

Independent counting gives

```text
deg_R0(T) =  20 + q(T)
deg_R1(T) = 180 - 3q(T)
deg_R2(T) =       3q(T)
deg_R3(T) =  12 - q(T)
```

At the frozen endpoint, `|E(R2)|=708`, so

```text
3 sum_T q(T) = 2*708
sum_T q(T)   = 472
sum_T deg_R3(T) = 231*12 - 472 = 2300
|E(R3)| = 1150.
```

No transitivity, constant-`q`, or automorphism assumption is used.

### Lemma 3: fixed-pair multiplicity and mixed-trace normalization

Agreement: `PASS`.

For two fixed transversals, cross-fibre matching edges cannot run between the
two transversals. Their relation is therefore exactly the number
`m_T(x,y)` of the three transported within-fibre matchings containing the
fixed-label pair. A forbidden local closure is `m_T(x,y)=2`.

The matrix calculation for one unordered closure is

```text
tr(A_R2 A_R3^2) = 2,
```

one contribution for each orientation of the `R2` edge. A hostile
one-orientation mutation gives `1`, confirming that the candidate's factor of
two is necessary and correct. Multiplicity three is an `R3` triangle, not a
forbidden double overlap.

### Lemma 4: projector uniqueness and endpoint cap

Agreement: `PASS_SCOPED`.

Three pairwise-`R3` marked rows have Gram matrix

```text
[ 4 -2 -2 ]
[-2  4 -2 ]
[-2 -2  4 ],
```

whose kernel is generated by `(1,1,1)`. Thus a common `R3` completion of an
`R3` edge has marked vector `-(X+Y)`.

The row-injectivity step also survives attack. A marked row has diagonal
inner product `4`; a distinct disjoint triangle has off-diagonal entry at
most `1`, a one-vertex overlap has entry at most `0`, and two distinct
triangles cannot share an edge when `lambda=1`. Hence two distinct triangle
rows cannot represent the same vector.

An `R3` edge consequently lies in at most one `R3` triangle. The 1,150
`R3` edges support at most `floor(1150/3)=383` edge-disjoint `R3` triangles,
or at most `3*383=1149` central triple-overlap events.

This is only an upper cap. It supplies no positive lower bound on triple
overlaps and no positive lower bound on forbidden double overlaps.
