## 3. Exact trace-square floor

Write the integral coordinate endomorphism as

```text
B=I+2C.
```

At the endpoint,

```text
tr(C)=(48-44)/2=2.
```

For every integral square matrix,

```text
tr(C^2)
 = sum_i C_ii^2 + 2 sum_(i<j) C_ij C_ji
 = tr(C) (mod 2).
```

Self-adjointness of `C` with respect to the positive Gram form makes it
similar over the reals to a symmetric matrix.  Its eigenvalues are real.
Because `tr(C)=2`, it is nonzero, and therefore

```text
tr(C^2)=sum_i lambda_i(C)^2
```

is a positive even integer.  Hence `tr(C^2)>=2`, and

```text
tr(B^2)
 =tr(I+4C+4C^2)
 >=44+8+8
 =60.
```

This argument does not require `C` to be symmetric in the chosen integral
basis; positive-form self-adjointness is sufficient.

## 4. Global determinant maximum: every boundary

Let `x_1,...,x_44` be the positive real eigenvalues of `B`.  They satisfy

```text
sum x_i=48,
sum x_i^2>=60.
```

Consider the compact set with `x_i>=0` and these two constraints.  The point

```text
3,3,1^42
```

is feasible and has product nine.  Therefore a product maximizer has
positive product and cannot lie on any coordinate boundary `x_i=0`.

If the maximizing square sum were strictly greater than 60, that inequality
would be locally inactive.  Stationarity of `sum log(x_i)` subject only to
`sum x_i=48` would force all coordinates equal.  The equal point has

```text
sum x_i^2=44(48/44)^2=576/11<60,
```

so it is not feasible.  Consequently every maximizer lies on the active
boundary `sum x_i^2=60`.

The two active constraint gradients are independent: dependence would make
all `x_i` equal, again contradicting the second moment.  Lagrange
stationarity therefore gives

```text
1/x_i=alpha+beta x_i.
```

Every coordinate is a positive root of one quadratic.  There cannot be only
one value, so there are exactly two.  If the larger value occurs `k` times,
the moment equations give

```text
a = [12+sqrt(21(44-k)/k)]/11,
b = [12-sqrt(21k/(44-k))]/11.
```

The exact condition `b>0` is

```text
144(44-k)>21k,
```

which gives precisely `k=1,...,38`.  Counts 39 through 43 are not missing
boundary cases; their smaller root is nonpositive.

The independent checker encloses both radicals in exact dyadic intervals.
For `a` it uses the upper radical endpoint, and for `b` the lower radical
endpoint, producing an exact rational upper bound on
`a^k b^(44-k)`.  All 38 bounds are strictly below 13.  The largest is the
`k=1` bound, approximately

```text

```

The decimal is informational; the stored numerator/denominator comparison

```text

```

```text

```

The even rank-44 Gram congruence is independently applicable:

```text
det(Q)=(-1)^22=1 mod 4.
```

Indeed, reduce the nonsingular alternating Gram matrix modulo two to
hyperbolic blocks, lift the change of basis integrally, and write
`Q=J+2R (mod 4)`.  Then

```text
det(Q)=det(J)(1+2tr(JR))=(-1)^22=1 (mod 4),
```

because `R` is symmetric and `tr(JR)` is even.  Van der Blij excludes
`det(Q)=1` in positive rank 44, so the floor is genuinely five.

strict new cap, equivalently integer cap 12, the only factor pairs are

Thus the two-moment argument eliminates `h=9`; `h=1` is the sole arithmetic
survivor.

## 6. The `h=1` rescaling obstruction

If `h=[L:21L*]=1`, the verified inclusion is equality:

```text
L=21L*.
```

For an integral basis with Gram matrix `G`, dual coordinates give

```text
21G^{-1}Z^44=Z^44.
```

Thus `21G^{-1}` is integral unimodular, and its inverse

```text
H=G/21
```

is integral unimodular.  It is symmetric positive definite.  Since `G` is
even and 21 is odd, the diagonal of `H` is even.  Therefore `H` defines an
even positive-definite unimodular integral lattice of rank 44.

The even-unimodular signature theorem requires the signature to be divisible
by eight, but `44=4 mod 8`.  Hence `h=1` is impossible.  The theorem's
hypotheses and frozen source attribution are detailed in
`theorem-applicability.md`.

No endpoint index remains, proving `n3!=705`.

## 7. Smith and modular side claims

Because

```text

```

the 44 nonzero Smith factors of `M`, viewed relative to the saturated image
`L`, divide 21 and lie in `{1,3,7,21}`.  Their product is
`[L:MZ^231]=h`.  Reducing Smith normal form modulo three and seven gives

```text

```

Thus `h=1` corresponds to ranks `(44,44)`, while `h=9` corresponds to
`(42,44)`.  These bookkeeping claims pass but are not needed to determine
either modular rank directly.

The characteristic-seven side claims also pass.  From
`NN^T=A+7I`, a vector in `ker(N^T)` lies in `ker(A mod 7)`.  The SRG
identity forces that kernel to be the constants, while
`N^T1=3*1` is nonzero.  Hence `N` has full row rank modulo seven.  With

```text
M=N^T(3I-A+J/9)N,
5(3I-A+J/9)=2A-J+I (mod 7),
(2A-J+I)^2=49(I+J),
```

the submitted rank reduction is exact, while the square-zero reduction does
not determine the rank.

For completeness, the stated characteristic-three rank is also correct,
although the candidate report only labels it verified.  Put `H=A+I`.
Modulo three,

```text
charpoly(H)=t^45(t-1)^54,
H^2-H=-J,
HJ=JH=0.
```

Let `V=V_0 direct-sum V_1` be the primary decomposition for the relatively
prime factors `t^45` and `(t-1)^54`.  On `V_1`, `H` is invertible, so
`JH=0` implies `J|V_1=0`; then `H(H-I)=-J` implies `H=I` there.  Thus this
space contributes rank 54.  On `V_0`, `H-I` is invertible, and

```text
H=-J(H-I)^(-1).
```

The rank-one map `J` vanishes on `V_1`, hence has rank one on `V_0`;
right multiplication by the invertible `H-I` shows that `H|V_0` also has
rank one.  Thus `rank_F3(A+I)=55`, and

```text

```
