## 2. Primitive lattice and dual/index convention

Let `U=im(M)` over the rationals, `E=M/21`, and
`L=U intersect Z^231`.  Since `U` is rational, `L` is a full lattice in
`U`; it is primitive because `kz in U` with nonzero integer `k` implies
`z in U`.

For `z in Z^231` and `x in L`,

```text
<Ez,x> = <z,x> in Z,
```

so `EZ^231` lies in `L*`.  Conversely, primitivity makes `L` a direct
summand of the free abelian group `Z^231`.  Every integral functional on
`L` therefore extends to an integral functional on `Z^231`; orthogonal
projection of its ambient representing vector gives the original dual
vector.  Thus, with no missing saturation factor,

```text
EZ^231 = L*,
MZ^231 = 21L*.
```

The right-hand lattice is integral because `M` is integral, so

```text
21L subset 21L* subset L.
```

Writing `d=det(X^T X)` for an integral basis matrix `X` of `L`, covolumes
give

```text
h=[L:21L*]
 = covol(21L*)/covol(L)
 = 21^44/d.
```

The exponent is 44, and the direction is not reciprocal.  The independent
checker also tests primitive rational lines where the index can be read off
directly.  Since `h` is an integer dividing `21^44`, it is 3,7-smooth.

## 3. Evenness, minimum, and binary rank

For `x in L`, `Mx=21x`.  The diagonal of `M` is even, so
`x^T M x` is even.  Reducing the equality
`x^T M x=21 x^T x` modulo two proves that `L` is even.

An integral norm-two vector has two unit coordinates.  Because `U` is
orthogonal to the all-ones vector, such a vector in `L` would be
`±(e_i-e_j)`.  But

```text
||E(e_i-e_j)||^2 = (8-2M_ij)/21 <= 12/21 < 2,
```

contradicting invariance under projection for a vector already in `U`.
The submitted minimum-at-least-four statement is correct and unused by the
endpoint determinant argument.

The inherited exact premise `rank_F2(M)=44` is applicable.  Since
`A=MWM` satisfies `A mod 2=M`, its binary rank is 44.  Integer rank is at
least binary rank, while `rank_R(A)<=rank_R(M)=44`; hence `rank_R(A)=44`.
As `A` is positive semidefinite and supported on the 44-space `U`, it is
positive definite on `U`.

## 4. Integral endomorphism and modulo-four determinant

Define `B=A/21` as an operator on `U`.  For `x in L`,

```text
Bx = M W x in MZ^231 = 21L* subset L.
```

Thus `B` has an integral coordinate matrix on `L`, is self-adjoint, and is
positive definite.  Its trace is the ambient nonzero trace:

```text
tr(B)=tr(A)/21=4 Delta.
```

Because `W=M mod 2`,

```text
Bx = MWx = M^2x = x  (mod 2 Z^231).
```

Both sides lie in `L`.  Primitivity supplies the often-hidden last step
`L intersect 2Z^231=2L`, so the congruence is genuinely modulo `2L`.
Writing the integral coordinate matrix as `B=I+2C` is therefore valid.

At rank 44,

```text
tr(C)=(4Delta-44)/2=2Delta-22
```

is even, and determinant expansion modulo four gives

```text
det(B)=1+2tr(C)=1 (mod 4).
```

No ambient entrywise integrality of `A/21` was assumed.

## 5. Determinant factorization and theorem applicability

For an integral basis matrix `X` of `L`, put

```text
G=X^T X,
Q=X^T W X,
BX=XT.
```

For basis vectors `x,y in L`,

```text
<x,By>=<x,MWy>=<Mx,Wy>=21<x,Wy>,
```

so

```text
GT=21Q.
```

Taking determinants and substituting `det(G)=21^44/h` gives exactly

```text
det(B)=det(T)=h det(Q).
```

The factor is `h`, not `1/h`, and no power of 21 remains.  The matrix `Q`
is integral.  It is positive definite because
`v^TQv=<Xv,BXv>/21>0` for nonzero `v`.  It is even because `W` has even
diagonal and all off-diagonal quadratic terms occur twice.

The quantities `h` and `det(B)` are odd, hence `det(Q)` is odd.  If
`det(Q)=1`, then `Q` is the Gram matrix of an even, positive-definite,
unimodular integral lattice of rank and signature 44.  Van der Blij's
signature lemma applies with characteristic vector zero and requires its
signature to be divisible by eight.  Since `44=4 mod 8`, this is impossible.
The theorem requires no minimum-norm or embedding hypothesis.  Attribution
and stable sources are recorded in `theorem-sources.md`.

Therefore the submitted lower bound `det(Q)>=3` is valid.

## 7. Harmonic cubic

Represent `M` as a Gram matrix of norm-four vectors in dimension 44.  The
degree-three harmonic-tensor Gram kernel for equal squared norms four is

```text
t^3 - (3*4*4/(44+2))t
 = t^3-(24/23)t.
```

Multiplication by 23 gives the submitted positive-semidefinite integral
kernel

```text

```

At Gram values `4,1,0,-1,-2`, its entries are respectively
`1376,-1,0,1,-136`.  Substituting the complete row counts gives

```text

```


which rejects `q=11,12` and no other integer `q` in `[0,12]`.  This is a
correct local endpoint restriction, not a contradiction.

## 8. Traceless contraction and dynamic program

For row Gram vectors `x_i`, define

```text
Y_i=sum_j <x_i,x_j> (x_j tensor x_j).
```

Then

```text
||Y_i||^2=A_ii,
<Y_i,x_i tensor x_i>=sum_j M_ij^3=6(q(i)-2).
```

The trace is `4 sum_j M_ij=0`, so `Y_i` is traceless.  The traceless part
of `x_i tensor x_i` has squared norm

```text
16-16/44=172/11.
```

Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives exactly

```text
A_ii >= 36(q-2)^2/(172/11)
     = (99/43)(q-2)^2.
```

floors.  The independent witness-producing dynamic program, over 231
