## 2. The scaled dual is integral, even, and positive definite

Let `X` be an integral basis matrix for
`L=U intersect Z^231`, and let `G=X^T X`.  In these coordinates,

```text
L*=G^{-1}Z^44.
```

The frozen inclusion `21L* subset L` therefore says exactly that

```text
S=21G^{-1}
```

is integral.  It is symmetric and positive definite.  Its determinant is

```text
det(S)=21^44/det(G)=h.
```

Because `h det(G)=21^44`, both `h` and `det(G)` are odd.  For each diagonal
entry, the adjugate formula gives

```text
S_ii det(G)=21 det(G with row i and column i deleted).
```

The 43-by-43 principal matrix on the right is symmetric with even diagonal.
Modulo two it represents an alternating form of odd dimension.  Alternating
forms have even rank, so its determinant vanishes modulo two.  The right
side is even; since `det(G)` is odd, `S_ii` is even.

Thus `S` is an even integral positive-definite Gram matrix of rank 44 and
odd determinant `h`.

For an even integral rank-`2m` Gram matrix with odd determinant,

```text
det=(-1)^m (mod 4).
```

The proof is frozen in `theorem-applicability.md`.  At rank 44 this yields

```text
h=det(S)=1 (mod 4).
```

If `h=1`, then `S` is even, positive definite, and unimodular of signature
44.  The even-unimodular signature theorem requires a multiple of eight,
whereas `44=4 mod 8`.  Hence

```text

```

## 3. Trace-square residue and exact Cauchy floor

`B=I mod 2 End(L)`, write its integral coordinate matrix as

```text

```

Then

```text

```

For every integral square matrix,

```text
tr(C^2)
 =sum_i C_ii^2+2sum_(i<j)C_ij C_ji

```

Consequently `tr(C^2)` is even and

```text

 =44+4tr(C)+4tr(C^2)
```


