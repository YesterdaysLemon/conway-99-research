## 3. Characteristic pseudodeterminant

Positive-form self-adjointness makes `C` similar over the reals to a symmetric
matrix, so it is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues.  If `r=rank(C)`, then

```text
char_C(t)=t^(44-r)p(t)
```

for a monic integer polynomial with nonzero integer constant.  Thus the
absolute product of the nonzero eigenvalues is at least one.  AM-GM on their
squares and Cauchy on their sum yield

```text
tr(C^2)>=r,
tr(C^2)>=64/r.
```

The exact enumeration of all `r=1,...,44` has unique minimum eight at `r=8`.
Consequently

```text

tr(B^2)>=44+32+32=108.
```

The control `C=(2/11)I_44` has trace eight and trace square `16/11`; it
demonstrates that integrality is active.

## 4. Logarithmic determinant cap

Put `L=log(3)` and `c=L-2/3`.  An exact rational partial sum and geometric
tail for `log(3)=2 atanh(1/2)` prove `2/3<L<2`.

For positive `x`, the derivative numerator of

```text
F(x)=xL-c log(x)-log(1+2x)
```

factors exactly as

```text
x(1+2x)F'(x)=(x-1)(2Lx+c).
```

The second factor is positive, and `F(1)=0`; hence `F>=0`.  For
`-1/2<x<0`,

```text
g'(x)=L-2/(1+2x)<L-2<0,
```

so `g(x)=xL-log(1+2x)>0`, while `-c log|x|>0`.  The pointwise inequality
therefore holds on the entire required nonzero domain:

```text
log(1+2x)<=x log(3)-c log|x|.
```

Zero eigenvalues contribute a factor one.  Summation, `tr(C)=8`, and the
nonzero integral characteristic coefficient now give

```text
log det(B)<=8 log(3),
det(B)<=3^8=6561.
```

All domain and theorem hypotheses are explicit in
`theorem-applicability.md`.  An integral skew-block control has trace eight
and determinant `17*6561`, but fails self-adjointness and has nonreal
eigenvalues; it detects an illicit omission of that hypothesis.
