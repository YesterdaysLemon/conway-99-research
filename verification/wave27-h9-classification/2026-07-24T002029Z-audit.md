## 2. Positivity without a symmetry shortcut

Let

```text
S=E6,
B=S Q,
C=(B-I)/2.
```

Although `S` and `Q` are symmetric, `B=SQ` need not be symmetric and the
proof does not treat it as symmetric.  Because both factors are real
positive definite,

```text
B is similar to S^(1/2) Q S^(1/2).
```

Thus `B` is diagonalizable with six positive real eigenvalues.  It also
satisfies

```text
B^T Q = Q S Q = Q B,
```

so `B`, and therefore `C`, is self-adjoint for the positive-definite
`Q`-inner product.  These facts justify:

- applying AM-GM and KKT to the six positive eigenvalues of `B`;
- identifying `tr(B^2)` with the sum of their squares;
- using real eigenvalues and semisimplicity in the pseudodeterminant step;
- splitting distinct eigenspaces orthogonally for `Q`.

No Euclidean symmetry, simultaneous Euclidean diagonalization, or
commutativity is needed.

## 3. Congruence and the traces below ten

The congruence `B=I (mod 2)` makes `det(B)` odd.  Since `det(S)=3`,
`det(Q)` is odd.  An even integral symmetric form of rank `2m` and odd
determinant has determinant `(-1)^m (mod 4)`.  At rank six this gives

```text
det(Q)=3 (mod 4),
det(B)=3 det(Q)=1 (mod 4).
```

For integral `C`,

```text
det(I+2C)=1+2tr(C) (mod 4).
```

Hence `tr(C)` is even and

```text
T=tr(B)=6+2tr(C)=2 (mod 4).
```

Positive definiteness and the determinant residue give `det(Q)>=3`, so
`det(B)>=9`.  If `T<=8`, AM-GM on the positive eigenvalues gives

```text
det(B) <= (T/6)^6 <= (8/6)^6
       = 4096/729
       < 9,
```

a contradiction.  This excludes the congruence values two and six without
any integrality restriction on an eigenbasis.

## 4. The `T=10` pseudodeterminant branch

Assume `T=10`; then `tr(C)=2`.  Since `C` is an integral diagonalizable
matrix, the product of its nonzero eigenvalues is a nonzero integer: it is
the first nonzero characteristic coefficient after the zero factor.

Write `r=rank(C)`.  If `tr(C^2)=1`, Cauchy gives `r>=4`, while AM-GM on the
squared nonzero eigenvalues gives

```text
|pseudodet(C)|^2 <= (1/r)^r < 1.
```

This contradicts nonzero integrality.  Therefore `tr(C^2)>=2`.

If equality holds, Cauchy gives `r>=2`.  For every `r>=3`,

```text
|pseudodet(C)|^2 <= (2/r)^r < 1,
```

again impossible.  Thus `r=2`, and the two nonzero real eigenvalues obey

```text
x+y=2,
x^2+y^2=2.
```

Consequently `x=y=1`.  Diagonalizability then gives `C^2=C`, so `C` is an
integral idempotent of rank two.

## 5. The rank-four kernel obstruction and the omitted parity bridge

An integral idempotent splits the full integer module:

```text
Z^6 = im(C) direct_sum ker(C).
```

Choose a unimodular matrix `U` whose columns are adapted to this split.
The relevant transformations are not the same congruence for both factors:

```text
C' = U^(-1) C U = diag(I2,0_4),
B' = U^(-1) B U = diag(3I2,I4),
Q' = U^T Q U,
S' = U^(-1) S U^(-T).
```

They satisfy `B'=S'Q'`.  Both `S'` and `Q'` remain symmetric, even integral,
and positive definite.  From `B^TQ=QB` one obtains
`B'^TQ'=Q'B'`; the distinct eigenvalues three and one force `Q'` to be
block diagonal.  Then `S'=B'(Q')^(-1)` is block diagonal too.  On the
rank-four kernel:

```text
S0 Q0 = I4.
```

Thus `S0` and `Q0` are mutually inverse even integral positive-definite
forms.  They are unimodular.  This would produce a positive-definite even
unimodular lattice of rank and signature four, contradicting the frozen
signature-divisibility-by-eight theorem.  Therefore
`tr(C^2)=2` is impossible.

At this point the discovery prose jumps directly to `tr(C^2)>=4`.  The
needed parity identity is

```text
tr(C^2)
 = sum_i C_ii^2 + 2 sum_(i<j) C_ij C_ji
 = sum_i C_ii
 = tr(C)                         (mod 2).
```

Since `tr(C)=2`, `tr(C^2)` is even.  After values below two and value two
have been excluded, the correct floor is therefore four.  Expanding
`B=I+2C` now gives

```text
tr(B^2)
 = 6+4tr(C)+4tr(C^2)
 >= 6+8+16
 = 30.
```

## 6. Exact determinant maximization at `T=10`

Let the positive eigenvalues of `B` be `x1,...,x6`.  They obey

```text
sum xi=10,
sum xi^2>=30.
```

On the compact closure of this feasible slice, the product is zero if any
coordinate is zero, so a positive maximum has all coordinates positive.
If the square constraint were inactive, stationarity under the sum alone
would force all coordinates equal, whose square sum is `100/6<30`.
Therefore a positive maximum lies on `sum xi^2=30`.

The two constraint gradients are independent there.  Lagrange
stationarity for the logarithm of the product gives a quadratic equation
for every `xi`, hence at most two distinct positive values.  Label the
larger value and let its multiplicity be `k`.  Positivity leaves
`k=1,2,3`.  Exact substitution gives:

```text
k=1: 5
k=2: (4625-1000 sqrt(10))/729 < 5
k=3: 125/729 < 5.
```

For `k=4`, the smaller value is
`(5-2sqrt(10))/3<0`; `k=5` is also nonpositive.  Thus

```text
det(B) <= 5,
```

contradicting the already proved `det(B)>=9`.  Trace ten is impossible.
Because `T=2 (mod 4)`, the first remaining value is fourteen.
