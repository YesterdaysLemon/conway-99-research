## Forced edges really are distinct `U`-edges

This bridge is used repeatedly and was audited separately from the numerical
inequalities.

Fix a size-three point `P`.  The external parts of its six co-points are
pairwise disjoint:

- two co-points based at the same label cannot share an external label by
  linearity; and
- co-points based at different labels cannot share one, because they and
  `P` would meet pairwise at three distinct labels.

For a singleton petal or an `L`-empty size-three co-point `Q` based at `j`,
every cross-pair between `P-{j}` and `Q-{j}` is in `K` by complementarity.
Such a cross-pair cannot be owned by another point clique.  If a point `R`
owned the pair `ia`, then `P,Q,R` would meet pairwise at the distinct labels
`j,i,a`.  Hence every such cross-pair is in

```text
U = K - E(F),
```

where `F` is the union of point-clique edges.  Distinct external labels make
the resulting neighbours distinct.  The independent checker constructs
literal symbolic endpoints and edge sets and verifies their cardinalities;
it does not merely compare the final inequalities.

Removing endpoint distinctness admits four extra mixed-profile triples,
including `(1,1,1)`.  Counting only one rather than two forced `U`-neighbours
per empty size-three crossing admits four extra `r=15` local modes.  These
mutation witnesses confirm that neither coefficient is dispensable.

## Elimination of the mixed `r=14` profile

### Point sizes

Expansion gives `2s<=14-s`, hence `s<=4`.  A size-four point has eight
pairwise-disjoint petals in ten external labels.  At least six petals have a
singleton external part.  Each of the six distinct endpoints is
`K`-adjacent to all four roots, giving every root at least

```text
3+6=9
```

`K`-neighbours, above the mixed profile's maximum seven.  Thus all points
have size two or three.

For a label `i`, let `t_i` be the number of size-three points through it.
Linearity makes all incident point-clique edges distinct, so

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=4-t_i  for q(i)=2,
d_U(i)=1-t_i  for q(i)=3.                       (1)
```

### A special label cannot lie in a size-three point

If `P={x,y,z}` contains a special label `x`, then `t_x=1` and
`d_U(x)=0`.  The forced-neighbour inequality at `x` is

```text
0 >= (3-t_y)+(3-t_z),
```

so `t_y=t_z=3`; both are ordinary.  The two size-two petals at `x` have
distinct endpoints and force two distinct `U`-neighbours at `y`, but (1)
gives `d_U(y)=1`.  This is a contradiction.

### Exact ordinary modes

For an ordinary size-three point, distinct forced neighbours give, cyclically,

```text

```

The disjoint external parts use

```text
sum_i ((3-t_i)+2(t_i-1)) = t_i+t_j+t_k+3
```

of the eleven outside labels, hence `t_i+t_j+t_k<=8`.  Exhaustive enumeration
of `t_i in {1,2,3}` leaves only

```text

```

In mode `(2,3,3)`, the unique size-two petal at the `t=2` root forces one
`U`-edge at each `t=3` root, saturating their one-edge capacities.  Its
external endpoint cannot be special: it already has the two distinct forced
edges to those roots, while a special label has `U`-degree at most one.

There are five other size-three co-points: one at the `t=2` root and two at
each `t=3` root.  Each has a saturated label on the `P` side of its
`2`-by-`2` crossing.  An empty crossing would force new `U`-edges there, so
all five crossings are full.  The co-points are distinct by linearity.  Each
represents an actual graph neighbour of the vertex represented by `P`,
because it shares a graph-triangle with `P`.  Thus the five terms contribute

### Mode `(2,2,2)` and the intersection graph

If a size-three point remains, every label in one has `t=2`.  Form the graph

- simple by point-family linearity;
- cubic because every size-three point has three distinct shared labels; and

## The all-`q=2`, `r=15` profile

Here `K` is 8-regular and every nonempty point `P=S_u` satisfies

```text

```

### Sizes five and four

Expansion gives `s<=5`.  For `s=5`, ten petals occupy ten outside labels,
so all are size two.  Their ten distinct endpoints are adjacent to every
root; each root has at least `4+10=14` `K`-neighbours.

For `s=4`, eight petals occupy eleven outside labels.  At least five have a
singleton external part.  If there are at least six, every root has at least
`3+6=9` neighbours.  If there are exactly five, any one of the other three
petals has at least two external labels, disjoint from the five common
endpoints.  Its base root has at least `3+5+2=10` neighbours.  Both cases
contradict degree eight.  All points therefore have size two or three.


### The `t=3` parity step

If a label `x` has `t_x=3`, each of its three size-three points is in the
last row of the table.  At `x`, exactly one of its two crossings with the
other points is empty.  Emptiness is a symmetric property of the same
undirected labeled crossing, so the empty pairs form a simple undirected
graph on three point objects in which every degree is one.  Exhaustive
enumeration gives zero such graphs.

Treating emptiness as directed would incorrectly admit a directed 3-cycle;
the mutation lane retains this witness.  The proof uses the correct symmetric
relation.  Hence no label has `t=3`, every size-three point has mode `111`,
`122`, or `222`, and every crossing between meeting size-three points is full.

### Mode `111`: every actual-neighbour class

Let `P={i,j,k}` have mode `111`.  Its six size-two petals have six distinct
endpoints `A`.  Each endpoint is adjacent in `K` to all three roots.
Consequently each root's eight neighbours are exactly

```text

```

Let `Z` be the remaining six active labels.  Every `z in Z` has all three
`L`-edges into `P`.

For the original graph vertex `u` represented by `P`, every possible actual
graph neighbour `v` is covered as follows:

1. If `S_v` meets `P`, it is one of the six size-two petals.  After deleting

2. If `S_v` is disjoint from `P` and contains `z in Z`, the row for `z` has

3. If `S_v` is disjoint and contained in `A`, every cross-pair is in `K`, so

4. If `S_v` is inactive, its crossing is empty.

### Exhaustive final crossing classification

Only size-two points, size-three points of modes `122/222`, and inactive
points remain.  Exhaustive enumeration of all simple bipartite crossing
matrices with every row and column degree in `{0,2}` gives:

| endpoint sizes | overlap crossing sizes | disjoint crossing sizes |
