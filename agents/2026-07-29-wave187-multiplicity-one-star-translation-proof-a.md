## 1. Frozen setting

Assume the independently verified conditional endpoint model

```text
n3=4158, P=0, rank_F3(D)=11.
```

Use the verified Waves 174, 176--180, and 186 facts:

1. each seven-column point-star has exactly one relation, supported on all
   seven coordinates;
2. the dual distance is at least four;
3. every nonedge is cross-realized by a circuit of weight at most nine;
4. a short circuit cross-realizes at most three nonedges;
5. exact multiplicities two and three have the Wave 181 and Wave 180
   classifications used by Wave 186; and
6. Wave 186 gives `Q>=3696` by private-label assignments for
   multiplicities two and three.

All circuits below are projective circuit supports.

## 2. The affine star-coset picture

Let a selected circuit `C_0` cross-realize exactly one nonedge `xy`.  The
stars at `x` and `y` are disjoint.  Write

```text
c in F_3^14
```

for its true relation, and let `s_x,s_y` be the two full-star relations.
The nine words

```text
c+alpha*s_x+beta*s_y,
alpha,beta in F_3,                                (2)
```

form an affine two-plane in the true relation code.

Suppose the support of `c` has side sizes

```text
a on star(x), b on star(y),  a+b<=9.              (3)
```

Both sides are nonempty because `C_0` cross-realizes `xy`.  Also

```text
1<=a,b<=6.                                        (4)
```

Indeed, if one side contained all seven star coordinates, the corresponding
full-star circuit would be a proper dependent subset of `C_0`, contrary to
minimality.

On the `x` side, let the two nonzero coefficient multiplicities of `c` be

```text
p_x,q_x,  p_x+q_x=a,
M_x=max(p_x,q_x).
```

The three possible `x`-side weights in (2) are

```text
a, 7-p_x, 7-q_x.                                  (5)
```

The first is the untranslated side.  A nonzero star scalar cancels one
coefficient sign, activates every formerly zero coordinate, and leaves the
opposite sign nonzero.  Therefore the best nonzero `x`-translation has
side weight `7-M_x`.  Define `M_y` analogously.

As a check on the additive structure, each side's three weights sum to
fourteen:

```text
a+(7-p_x)+(7-q_x)=14.
```

Consequently the total weight of all nine affine words is

```text
3*14+3*14=84,
```

with exact average `28/3`.  This average alone does not distinguish the
already selected circuit from a new one; the next majority argument does.

## 3. A short nontrivial axis translate always exists

Cancel a majority coefficient sign at `x`.  The resulting relation has
weight

```text
w_x=7-M_x+b.                                      (6)
```

The analogous `y`-translation has weight

```text
w_y=a+7-M_y.                                      (7)
```

Suppose both were at least ten.  Since all quantities are integral,

```text
M_x<=b-3,
M_y<=a-3.                                         (8)
```

But `M_x>=a/2` and `M_y>=b/2`.  Equations (8) would give

```text
a<=2*b-6,
b<=2*a-6.
```

Adding these inequalities yields

```text
a+b>=12,
```

contrary to (3).  Hence

```text
min(w_x,w_y)<=9.                                  (9)
```

This is the exact singleton translation lemma.  It uses the coefficient
signs, not only the support sizes.

## 4. Circuit extraction and privacy

Choose an endpoint translation supplied by (9), and call its support `W`.
On the translated side, at least one occupied coordinate was canceled, so
that side has at most six coordinates.  The untranslated side also has at
most six by (4).  Thus neither side of `W` contains its full-star circuit.

The dependent set `W` contains a circuit `D`.  A one-sided circuit is
impossible because every proper subset of either star is independent.
Therefore `D` uses both stars and cross-realizes `xy`.  Its weight lies in

```text
4..9
```

by dual distance and (9).

The translation canceled at least one coordinate of `C_0`, so
`supp(C_0)` is not contained in `W`.  Hence `D` is distinct from `C_0`.

In an inclusion-minimal cover, the sole label of a multiplicity-one
selected circuit is private.  Any distinct circuit serving that label lies
outside the selected cover.  Therefore:

```text
each selected multiplicity-one circuit forces
at least one outside short circuit serving its label.             (10)
```

The outside circuit in (10) may coincide with a Wave 180 companion or a
translate already assigned to another private label.  No global
distinctness is claimed; Wave 179's capacity-three bound controls exactly
this reuse.

## 5. Refined private-label accounting

Retain the Wave 186 notation:

```text
L=4158,
N=n_1+n_2+n_3,
S=n_1+2*n_2+3*n_3.
```

Let `P_priv` be the number of nonedges covered exactly once by an
inclusion-minimal cover.  Wave 186 proves

```text
P_priv>=2*L-S.                                    (11)
```

The `n_1` singleton labels are private.  Put

```text
p=P_priv-n_1.
```

These are the private labels owned by multiplicity-two and
multiplicity-three selected circuits.  Wave 186 supplies two distinct
outside assignments for each of those `p` labels.  Equation (10) supplies
one for each singleton label.  If `O` denotes all nonedge-realizing short
circuits outside the selected cover, capacity three gives

```text
3*O>=n_1+2*p
    =2*P_priv-n_1
   >=4*L-3*n_1-4*n_2-6*n_3.                     (12)
```

The verified distinct companions of selected multiplicity-three circuits
still give

```text
O>=n_3.                                           (13)
```

The two counts concern the same outside pool; they are not added as
disjoint families.

## 6. Exact scalar consequence


Multiply (13) by three:


Adding (14)--(15) gives

```text
9*O+6*n_1+8*n_2+9*n_3>=8*L.
```

```text
 >=9*(O+n_1+n_2+n_3)
```

Since `8*4158/9=3696`, (16) is exactly (1):


In particular:

```text

Q=3696     implies n_1=n_2=0.                    (18)
```

No theorem in the frozen inputs excludes such a triple-only cover.

## 7. Three short projective words in every affine orbit

The circuit-extraction collision does not occur if the affine words
themselves are counted.  Let the three side weights from (5), in increasing
order, be

```text
u_1<=u_2<=u_3
```

and define `v_1<=v_2<=v_3` on the other side.  Every entry is positive and
at most seven, and

```text
u_1+u_2+u_3=v_1+v_2+v_3=14.                     (20)
```

At least three of the nine sums `u_i+v_j` are at most nine.  If
`u_1<=2`, the three sums `u_1+v_j` work because `v_j<=7`; the case
`v_1<=2` is symmetric.  Otherwise both minima are at least three.  Equation
(20) then gives

```text
u_1,v_1<=4,  u_2,v_2<=5,
```

so

```text
u_1+v_1<=8,
u_1+v_2<=9,
u_2+v_1<=9.                                      (21)
```

The nine affine words are also projectively distinct.  Modulo

```text
H=span(s_x,s_y),
```

each has class `[c]`.  This class is nonzero: a word in `H` has a full
seven-star side, whereas the circuit `c` has two proper nonempty sides.  If
two affine words differed by projective scalar `lambda`, reduction modulo
`H` would give

```text
[c]=lambda*[c].
```

Thus `lambda=1`; independence of the two disjoint star relations then
forces the affine parameters to agree.

The selected circuit occupies one short cell.  Equations (20)--(21)
therefore prove:

```text
every selected short cross circuit has at least two other
projectively distinct dual words of weights 4..9 in its orbit.   (22)
```

The lower endpoint four in (22) is the verified dual-distance bound.

## 8. Support-level multiplicity of the alternative words

This section classifies cross-realization of a short **word support**.  It
does not assume that the support is a circuit.

Let a nonzero word of weight at most nine be supported in the disjoint stars
`S_x union S_y`, cross-realize `xy`, and have at least two support blocks on
each side.  A second realizing pair sharing `x`, say `xz`, would force every
one of the at least two `y`-side blocks to contain `z`.  Two graph triangles
would then contain the edge `yz`, contradicting `lambda=1`.  The case
sharing `y` is symmetric.

If a second realizing pair is disjoint from `xy`, every support block has
one of only four endpoint-pair patterns.  Uniqueness of a triangle through
a graph edge gives at most one block per pattern, so the word has weight at
most four.  Dual distance gives weight exactly four.  The Wave 179
four-pattern argument, which is purely about the support, excludes a third
realizing pair.  Hence

```text
both sides at least two:
  weight >4  => exact cross-realization multiplicity 1,
  weight  4  => cross-realization multiplicity at most 2.        (23)
```

It remains to classify a one-block side.  Suppose the sole `y`-side block
is

```text
T={y,u,v}.
```

After normalizing its nonzero coefficient, the relation expresses
`z_T in E_x`.  The vertex `x` cannot be adjacent to both `u,v`, since then
the edge `uv` would have the two common neighbors `x,y`.

Suppose `x` is adjacent to exactly one of them, say `u`.  The nonedges
`xy,xv` have distinct second common neighbors.  Their two outer `x`-star
blocks are distinct, since equality would create the forbidden triangular
prism.  The seven pairings of `z_T` against the `x`-star therefore have the
Wave 181 profile

```text
five 1s and two 2s.
```

But `z_T in E_x` and projector singularity give

```text
0=<z_T,P_x z_T>=-(5+2)=-7=2 in F_3,
```

a contradiction.  Thus `x` is anticomplete to `T`.

The Wave 180 local projector calculation now uses only
`x` anticomplete to `T` and `z_T in E_x`; circuit minimality is not needed.
The eight-column local relation space has exactly three projective words
involving `T`, of weights

```text
4, 5, 8.                                         (24)
```

All three cross-realize exactly the same nonedge triple

```text
{x,y}, {x,u}, {x,v}.                              (25)
```

The weight-eight member is nonminimal because it contains the full
seven-star circuit.  It is nevertheless a valid dual word and is precisely
why the word count is stronger than the circuit count.

## 9. Direct short-word amplification of a minimal cover

For an inclusion-minimal cover, let `p_i` denote the total number of private
labels belonging to selected multiplicity-`i` circuits.  Then

```text
p_1=n_1,  p_2>=n_2,  p_3>=n_3.                   (26)
```

Let `O_word` count nonedge-realizing projective dual words of weights
four through nine outside the selected circuit words.

### 9.1 Type-one selected circuits

Partition the `n_1` private labels according to whether some short
alternative in the affine orbit (22) has a one-block side.

For each unmarked label, choose two alternatives from (22).  Both have at
least two blocks per side, so (23) lets any one word serve at most two such
private labels.  Two assignments per label therefore force at least one
distinct outside word per unmarked label.

For each marked label, choose a one-block alternative.  Equations
(24)--(25) supply its entire three-word local orbit.  Every member lies
outside the selected cover because it serves the marked private label.
A fixed orbit can be marked by at most its three labels, so three words per
orbit force at least one distinct outside word per marked label.

The first pool has cross-realization multiplicity at most two; the second
has exact multiplicity three.  They are disjoint.  Therefore type one
contributes at least

```text
n_1 outside projective short words.               (27)
```

### 9.2 Type-two selected circuits

Fix a private label of a selected checkerboard conic.  Relative to that
label the coefficient-frequency profile on each star is `(5,1,1)`.
Adding either nonzero multiple of the first endpoint-star relation, or
either nonzero multiple of the second, gives four affine words, each of
weight eight and side profile `6+2` or `2+6`.

Section 7 makes the four words projectively distinct.  Equation (23) makes
each an exact-multiplicity-one word for the fixed private label.  They are
outside the cover by privacy, and words arising from different private
labels cannot coincide because their unique realizing labels differ.
Type two therefore contributes

```text
4*p_2 outside projective short words.             (28)
```

### 9.3 Type-three selected circuits

For each selected exact-three circuit, (24) consists of the weight-four
circuit, its weight-five circuit companion, and the nonminimal weight-eight
word.  Whichever circuit is selected, the other two words lie outside the
cover.  Different selected triple label sets give disjoint three-word
orbits, so this contributes `2*n_3`.

For each private label `{x,y_i}` on the selected triple, translate the
weight-four member by the nonzero `y_i`-star scalar that cancels `T`.
The resulting word has side profile `3+6` and weight nine.  Equation (23)
makes it an exact-multiplicity-one word for that private label.  These give
another `p_3` distinct outside words.  Thus type three contributes

```text
2*n_3+p_3 outside projective short words.         (29)
```

### 9.4 Disjointness and the scalar certificate

The exact-one words in (28)--(29) have different private labels from the
type-one pool.  A type-one word of exact multiplicity one therefore cannot
collide with them, and a type-one word of multiplicity two cannot collide
with them by multiplicity.  The exact-three orbit pool is disjoint from
all multiplicity-at-most-two pools.

A marked type-one triple orbit also cannot equal a selected type-three
orbit: the selected type-three circuit would then cover the supposedly
private type-one label.  The same privacy argument prevents collisions
with any selected cover word.  Consequently (27)--(29) combine without
overcounting:

```text
O_word>=n_1+4*p_2+p_3+2*n_3.                     (30)
```

Let `M` count all nonedge-realizing projective dual words of weights four
through nine.  The selected cover has `n_1+n_2+n_3` projective circuit
words and is disjoint from `O_word`, so

```text
M
 >=2*n_1+n_2+3*n_3+4*p_2+p_3.                   (31)
```

Private-label incidence gives

```text
n_1+p_2+p_3>=2*L-(n_1+2*n_2+3*n_3),

2*n_1+2*n_2+3*n_3+p_2+p_3>=2*L.                 (32)
```

Subtract the left side of (32) from the right side of (31).  The difference
is

```text
3*p_2-n_2>=2*n_2>=0                              (33)
```

by `p_2>=n_2`.  Therefore

```text
M>=2*L=8316.                                      (34)
```

The 693 verified edge-isolated projective circuits are disjoint from this
nonedge-realizing word family.  Hence there are at least

```text
8316+693=9009                                    (35)
```

projective dual-word classes of weights four through nine.  Every
projective ternary class has exactly two nonzero scalar representatives,
which proves

```text
B_4+B_5+B_6+B_7+B_8+B_9>=2*9009=18018.           (36)
```

## 10. Hostile routes and failed circuit strengthenings

### 10.1 The affine average alone is insufficient for circuits

The `28/3` average in Section 2 guarantees a short affine word, but the
original selected word already has weight at most nine.  The additive-grid
argument in Section 7 forces three short projective words.  It does not
force three distinct circuits after circuit extraction.

### 10.2 Two star scalars need not give two circuits

It is invalid to count both nonzero scalar translations at one endpoint as
distinct circuits.  Consider the exact formal relation-space control

```text
s_y=(0^7 | 1,1,1,1,1,1,1),
c  =(1,0^6 | 1,1,1,1,0,0,0).
```

Then

```text
d=c+2*s_y=(1,0^6 | 0,0,0,0,2,2,2)
```

has weight four, while `c+s_y` has weight eight and its support contains
`supp(d)`.  Circuit extraction from both translated supports may therefore
return the same cross circuit `d`.  The full affine relation space
`<s_x,s_y,c>` still has minimum nonzero weight four, so dual distance four
does not repair this distinctness failure.

This is a relation-space control only, not an endpoint graph or complete
code.  It does not challenge Section 7: `d` and `c+s_y` remain distinct
projective **words**, even though the latter is nonminimal.

### 10.3 A two-star translate need not remain short

For side profile `1+3` with one coefficient sign on each side, canceling
the occupied sign at both endpoints gives support size

```text
(7-1)+(7-3)=10.
```

Thus sums or differences of both star circuits do not universally stay
within the short range `4..9`.

### 10.4 Intersecting dependent supports do not identify their circuits

Outside the Wave 186 conic profile, two translated supports can intersect
in four or more coordinates.  Dual distance four then does not prevent
both from containing the same extracted circuit.  A dependent support is
not itself a projective circuit certificate.

No complete classification of the circuits inside all nine affine words
is proved here.  In particular, a second outside circuit per singleton
label remains `UNKNOWN`.

### 10.5 Singleton-only circuit gains cannot beat the triple-only row

Even if a stronger local theorem forced any fixed number `t` of outside
assignments for every singleton label, its contribution would be
proportional to `n_1`.  The Wave 186 hostile row has

```text
n_1=n_2=0.
```

It therefore survives every multiplicity-one-only scalar strengthening.
A uniform improvement beyond `3696` requires either:

1. a proof that every inclusion-minimal cover has `n_1+n_2>0`;
2. a stronger restriction on multiplicity-three companions or their
   capacity-three reuse; or
3. an independent complete short-weight upper bound.

The word-level argument avoids this circuit bottleneck by retaining the
nonminimal weight-eight member of every type-three local orbit and the
actual translated conic words instead of extracting circuits from them.

## 11. Boundary

The singleton affine-star lemma and inequality (16) are new conditional
necessary restrictions:

```text
9*Q>=8*4158+3*n_1+n_2.
```

They sharpen the equality structure but do not improve the unconditional
Wave 186 lower bound `Q>=3696`.  The exact unresolved cover target is now
a triple-only minimal cover with the Wave 186 scalar equality data.

At the distinct dual-word level, the independently checkable consequence
is the stronger necessary condition (36):

```text
B_4+...+B_9>=18018.
```

This is not a claim that `Q>=8316`: the count deliberately includes
nonminimal affine words.  No strict `n3` improvement, rank-11 exclusion,
graph construction, or Conway-99 resolution follows.  External novelty is
`UNKNOWN`.
