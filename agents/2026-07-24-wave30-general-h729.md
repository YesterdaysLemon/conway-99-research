## 2. Every frame row lies in one integral block

Refine a nontrivial integral orthogonal decomposition as

```text
S=S_1 orthogonal_sum ... orthogonal_sum S_m,
qquad m>=2.
```

Write a row of `X` in integral block coordinates:

```text

```

The endpoint diagonal says `x_i^T S x_i=4`.  Every nonzero component has
even norm at least four because `min(S)>=4`.  Thus exactly one component
of each row is nonzero.  After permuting rows, `X` is block diagonal.

The tight-frame identity then restricts to each block:

```text
X_j^T X_j=21 S_j^(-1).                         (2)
```

Multiplying by `S_j` and taking traces gives the exact shell count

```text

```

In particular every component rank is divisible by four.  Direct
multiplication and preservation of cross zeros under entrywise squaring
give

```text

W=orthogonal_sum W_j,
Q=orthogonal_sum Q_j,
B=orthogonal_sum B_j,
C=orthogonal_sum C_j.                           (4)
```

Thus the shell split, determinant allocation, trace calculation, and
logarithmic argument do not depend on the named `K12` or `LAMBDA(F)`
matrices.  They depend only on an integral minimum-four orthogonal split.
