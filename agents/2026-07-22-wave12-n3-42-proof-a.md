## Premise boundary

Assume a putative Conway graph has `n3=42`. Retain the Wave 6 graph `H`,
the Wave 7 graph `L` on graph-triangles, and the active complement

```text
K = complement(L[A])
```

on the active triangle set `A`. For every original graph vertex `u`, its
active point set `S_u` is a clique in `K`. The point family is linear, three
distinct point sets cannot pairwise meet at three distinct active triangles,
and every active triangle lies in exactly three point sets. There are no
singleton active point sets.

For two point sets meeting at active triangle `i`, delete the two labeled
copies of `i`. Every row and column of their remaining bipartite `L`-crossing
has degree zero or two. In particular, a crossing with a singleton side is
empty. Complementarity then turns every such absent `L`-edge into a `K`-edge.
The common-point rule prevents any resulting cross-edge from being owned by
a third point set, so it lies outside the union of the point-clique edges.

All claims below are conditional on these earlier premises. No connectedness
of `K`, transitivity, or automorphism of a completed graph is used.

## Exact active profiles

The established identities give

```text
q(T)=0 or q(T)>=2,
d_L(T)=3q(T),
sum_T q(T)=2n3/3=28.
```

If `r=|A|`, then `r<=14` and every active value obeys
`3q(T)<=r-1`. Exact partitioning leaves five values of `r` and six profiles:

| `r` | active `q` multiset | `K`-degree multiset |
|---:|---|---|
| 14 | `(2^14)` | `(7^14)` |
| 13 | `(2^12,4)` | `(6^12,0)` |
| 13 | `(2^11,3^2)` | `(6^11,3^2)` |
| 12 | `(2^8,3^4)` | `(5^8,2^4)` |
| 11 | `(2^5,3^6)` | `(4^5,1^6)` |
| 10 | `(2^2,3^8)` | `(3^2,0^8)` |

Three non-singleton point cliques through an active triangle consume at least

```text
r=14: (2^14)
r=13: (2^11,3^2)
```

survive that first test.

## No active triangle can have `K`-degree three

This local lemma eliminates the mixed `r=13` profile.

Suppose `d_K(x)=3`. Its three non-singleton point cliques consume three
distinct incident edges, so they are exactly

```text
{x,a}, {x,b}, {x,c},
N_K(x)={a,b,c}.
```

At active triangle `a`, let `P,Q` be its two other point sets. After deleting
`a`, the crossing of `{x,a}` with either `P` or `Q` has singleton side `{x}`,
so it is empty. Every member of `(P union Q)-{a}` is consequently adjacent
to `x` in `K`, and hence lies in `{b,c}`.

Linearity makes the two nonempty external parts disjoint. Therefore, up to
order,

```text
P={a,b}, Q={a,c}.
```

Repeating at `b` and `c` forces the three point sets

```text
{a,b}, {a,c}, {b,c}.
```

They pairwise meet at the three distinct active triangles `a,b,c`, violating
the common-point rule. Thus no active `K`-vertex has degree three. The two
degree-three vertices in `(6^11,3^2)` are impossible, leaving

```text
r=14, q(T)=2 for all active T, K is 7-regular.              (1)
```

## Expansion and exclusion of size four

Fix an active point set `P` of size `s`. At each `i in P`, the other two
point sets contribute nonempty external parts. Linearity separates the two
parts based at one `i`; a collision between parts based at distinct `i,j`
would make those two petals and `P` a forbidden Berge triangle. Selecting one
member of each part gives `2s` distinct labels outside `P`. Hence

```text
2s <= 14-s,
s <= 4.                                                     (2)
```

Suppose now `|P|=4`. Its eight petals have pairwise-disjoint nonempty external
parts among the ten labels outside `P`. By (2), a petal has external size one,
two, or three. If `h` petals have external size one, then

```text
10 >= h + 2(8-h) = 16-h,
h >= 6.
```

Thus at least six petals are size-two point sets. For such a petal `{i,a}`,
the singleton crossing `{a}` against `P-{i}` is empty. The endpoint `a` is
`K`-adjacent to all of `P`: to `i` by its point clique and to the other three
members by complementarity. The six endpoints are distinct. Every vertex of
`P` would therefore have at least

```text
3 internal + 6 external = 9
```

`K`-neighbors, contrary to (1). All active points have size two or three.

## `F/U` accounting

Let `F` be the union of all point-clique edges and set

```text
U = K-E(F).
```

If `t_i` is the number of size-three point sets through active triangle `i`,
then the three linear point cliques consume

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=4-t_i.                                               (3)
```

Consider a size-three point

```text
P={i,j,k}.
```

At root `j`, exactly `3-t_j` of the other point sets are size-two petals.
Each singleton endpoint has forced `U`-edges to both members of `P-{j}`.
All petal external parts around `P` are pairwise disjoint. Looking at the
forced neighbors of `i` coming from roots `j,k` gives

```text
4-t_i >= (3-t_j)+(3-t_k),                                  (4)
```

and the two cyclic companions.

The six petal external parts use

```text
sum_{r in P} ((3-t_r)+2(t_r-1))
  = 3 + t_i+t_j+t_k
```

of the eleven labels outside `P`. Therefore

```text
t_i+t_j+t_k <= 8.                                           (5)
```

Enumerating `t_i,t_j,t_k in {1,2,3}` under (4)-(5) leaves only

```text
(2,2,2) and permutations of (2,3,3).                        (6)
```

In particular a size-three point has no `223` occurrence in the local
point-size notation.

## Excluding the `(2,3,3)` propagation mode

Let `P={i,j,k}` have `t`-profile `(2,3,3)`, with `t_i=2`. At `i`, the other
points are a size-three point `Q` and a size-two point `{i,a}`. Singleton
crossings force

```text
aj,ak in U.
```

Since `d_U(j)=d_U(k)=1`, both `j` and `k` are saturated by the neighbor `a`.

The crossing between any two size-three points meeting at a root is a
`2`-by-`2` bipartite graph, so it is either empty in `L` or all of `K2,2`.
In the first case complementarity gives four `K`-edges, and the common-point
rule prevents any of them from lying in `F`; they are four `U`-edges.

At root `i`, this first case would add `U`-edges at the saturated `j,k`.
Thus the `P,Q` crossing is all of `K2,2` in `L`. At root `j`, the point `P`
meets two other size-three points. The external side `P-{j}` contains the
saturated vertex `k`, so both crossings must again be full in `L`. The same
argument at `k` gives two more full crossings.

Linearity makes these five co-points distinct. They are original graph
neighbors of the point object represented by `P`, because each shares an
active graph-triangle with it. The support identity assigns each graph edge
`H`-degree four. Their contribution is at least twenty, contradicting the
fixed-point identity

```text
sum_{v: Pv in E(G)} d_H(Pv) = 4|P| = 12.
```

The `(2,3,3)` mode is impossible. Every size-three point would have all three
of its labels at `t=2`.

## Cubic line-graph obstruction excludes every size-three point

Suppose at least one size-three point remains. Make a graph `R` whose vertices
are the size-three point sets. An active label with `t=2` joins the two
size-three points containing it.

Every size-three point contains three such labels, so `R` is cubic. Linearity
makes it simple. A triangle in `R` would give three pairwise point
intersections at three distinct active labels, because every label has
`t=2`; the common-point rule therefore makes `R` triangle-free.

Write

```text
m=|V(R)|,
e=|E(R)|=3m/2.
```

The `e` edge labels of `R` are exactly the active labels with `t=2`; the other
`14-e` active labels have `t=0`. At an edge label `i=PQ`, the third point is
a size-two set `{i,a_i}`. The singleton endpoint is adjacent in `U` to the
other two edge labels at `P` and the other two at `Q`. These four labels are
distinct, so (3) gives

```text
t_(a_i)=0,
N_U(a_i)=N_(L(R))(i).                                       (7)
```

The `e` size-two points `{i,a_i}` are distinct and consume `e` incidences
among the `3(14-e)` point incidences available at the `t=0` labels. Hence

```text
e <= 3(14-e),
e <= 10.                                                    (8)
```

A nonempty simple cubic graph has even order at least four. Equations (8) and
`e=3m/2` leave `m=4` or `m=6`. The order-four case is `K4`, contrary to
triangle-freeness. A triangle-free cubic graph on six vertices is `K3,3`:
its complement is 2-regular; a complementary `C6` gives the triangular prism,
while complementary `2C3` gives `K3,3`.

Thus the only possible `R` is `K3,3`, with nine edges and only five `t=0`
labels. But the open neighborhood of an edge in

```text
L(K3,3) = the 3-by-3 rook graph
```

uniquely identifies that edge. In cell notation `(r,c)`, it consists of the
other two cells in row `r` and the other two in column `c`. Therefore (7)
shows that

```text
i -> a_i
```

is injective: equal images would give equal open line-graph neighborhoods.
This would inject nine edge labels into five `t=0` labels, a contradiction.
No size-three point exists.

## The exact surviving frontier

All active point sets have size two. The 42 point incidences therefore form
exactly 21 point sets. Regard them as the edges of a graph `F` on the fourteen
active labels. Then:

1. `F` is simple cubic;
2. `F` is triangle-free by the common-point rule;
3. `F` is a spanning subgraph of `K`;
4. the three `F`-neighbors of every active label form a `K`-clique; and
5. with `U=K-E(F)`, `U` is 4-regular and contains every distance-two pair of
   `F`.

The last statement follows from the empty singleton crossing between each pair
of size-two points through one active triangle. It is a necessary local
reduction, not an existence result.

To continue, one must encode the global `H` support. A positive graph edge
between two size-two point objects has `H`-degree four and corresponds to an
`L`-complete rectangle between two disjoint `F`-edges. Every `F`-edge point
needs exactly two such support partners, and every `L`-edge needs exactly two
independent cross-edge rectangles. These global coverage conditions are not
implied by the five local bullets.

## Falsification record and computational boundary

The weaker local bullets genuinely have a countermodel. Let

```text
F = C7 square K2
```

be the 14-vertex heptagonal prism, take the 21 edges of `F` as the point sets,
and let `K` join vertices at `F`-distance one or two. Every vertex has three
distance-one and four distance-two neighbors, so `K` is 7-regular. The prism
is triangle-free, and every meeting singleton crossing is empty because its
two external endpoints have `F`-distance two. Thus this object satisfies the
point-hypergraph, complement, linearity, common-point, and meeting-crossing
premises used in the local reduction.

It is not an SRG candidate and it fails exact `H` support. In aligned prism
coordinates, the `L`-edge from top vertex `0` to bottom vertex `2` has only
one pair of incident `F`-edges whose four endpoint pairs all lie in `L`;
an `N3` side pair needs two independent cross graph edges. The local SAT
script found a labeled copy of this diagnostic object, and the focused support
script independently rejected that copy at the deficient `L`-edge.

The larger exploratory SAT model adds exact support degree, double coverage,
and side-matching constraints for an arbitrary all-size-two `F,K` pair. Its
first 120-second run returned no conclusion. This timeout is not evidence of
unsatisfiability and is not used anywhere in the proof.

The proof therefore stops at the all-size-two exact-support frontier. It does
not exclude `n3=42`, improve the numerical bound `n3>=42`, construct a Conway
graph, or prove nonexistence. The target remains `UNKNOWN`.
