## Premise boundary

Assume a putative Conway graph has `n3=45`. Retain the verified conditional
framework through Wave 12:

- `H` is the triangle-free graph on graph edges whose edges are the induced
  `N3` copies, so `|E(H)|=n3`;
- `L` is the graph on graph-triangles whose edges are the induced `N3`
  side-pairs;
- an active graph-triangle `T` has
  `q(T)>0`, with `q(T)=0` or `q(T)>=2`,
  `d_L(T)=3q(T)`, and
  `sum_T q(T)=2n3/3`;
- `K` is the simple complement of `L` on the active triangles;
- every original graph vertex `u` gives a point set
  `S_u` of active triangles containing `u`;
- the point family is linear, every active label lies in exactly three point
  sets, every active point has size at least two, and every point is a clique
  in `K`;
- three point sets cannot pairwise meet at three different active labels;
- for an actual graph edge `uv`, the labeled bipartite `L`-crossing between
  `S_u` and `S_v` has every non-overlap degree zero or two, and

```text
d_H(uv) = e_L(S_u,S_v),
sum_{v:uv in E(G)} d_H(uv) = 2 sum_{T in S_u} q(T).       (1)
```

The allowed degrees remain

```text
d_H(uv) in {0,4,6,8,10,12}.                              (2)
```

When two point sets meet at an active label, deleting the two labeled copies
of that label leaves the same degree-zero-or-two crossing. In particular, a
crossing with a singleton external side is empty. Complementarity makes all
of its cross-pairs `K`-edges. The common-point rule prevents such forced
cross-pairs from being owned by a third point, so they lie outside the union
of point-clique edges.

No premise below identifies active labels by an automorphism or assumes that
`K` is connected.

is short enough to repeat. If `d_K(x)=3`, the three points through `x` must be

```text
{x,a}, {x,b}, {x,c}.
```

At `a`, the singleton-side crossing with `{x,a}` forces both other nonempty
point parts into the two labels `{b,c}`. Linearity makes them `{a,b}` and
`{a,c}`. Repeating at `b` forces `{b,c}`. The three points
`{a,b},{a,c},{b,c}` meet pairwise at three different labels, contrary to the
common-point rule. Thus only the `r=14` and `r=15` profiles remain.

## The mixed `r=14` profile

Call the two `q=3` labels special. They have `K`-degree four; the other twelve
labels have `K`-degree seven.

### Points have size two or three

The standard flower expansion gives

```text
2|P| <= 14-|P|,
|P| <= 4.                                                   (4)
```

If `|P|=4`, its eight other point sets, or petals, have pairwise-disjoint
nonempty external parts in the ten labels outside `P`. If `h` petals have
singleton external part, then

```text
10 >= h+2(8-h)=16-h,
h>=6.
```

Every singleton endpoint is `K`-adjacent to all four labels of `P`. Hence
each label of `P` has at least `3+6=9` `K`-neighbors, exceeding even the
maximum degree seven. All points have size two or three.

Let `t_i` be the number of size-three points through label `i`, let `F` be
the union of all point-clique edges, and put `U=K-E(F)`. Then

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=4-t_i  if q(i)=2,
d_U(i)=1-t_i  if q(i)=3.                                  (5)
```

### No size-three point contains a special label

Suppose `P={x,y,z}` contains a special label `x`. Equation (5) gives
`t_x=1` and `d_U(x)=0`. The size-two petals based at `y,z` force

```text
0 >= (3-t_y)+(3-t_z),
```

so `t_y=t_z=3`. Neither can be special, since a special label has `t<=1`.
At the ordinary label `y`, however,

```text
d_U(y)=4-3=1
```

while the two size-two petals based at `x` already force two different
`U`-neighbors of `y`. This is a contradiction.

### No ordinary size-three point survives

For an ordinary size-three point `P={i,j,k}`, the same flower calculation as
in Wave 12 gives, cyclically,

```text
4-t_i >= (3-t_j)+(3-t_k),                                  (6)
t_i+t_j+t_k <= 8.                                          (7)
```

The only sorted possibilities are

```text
(2,2,2), (2,3,3).                                          (8)
```

The second mode is impossible. Let `t_i=2` and `t_j=t_k=3`, and write
`{i,a}` for the unique size-two co-point at `i`. Singleton crossings force
`aj,ak` into `U`, saturating the one available `U`-edge at each of `j,k`.
The endpoint `a` cannot be special, because a special label has only one
available `U`-edge.

The one other size-three point at `i` and the two at each of `j,k` must all
have full `2`-by-`2` `L`-crossing with `P`: an empty crossing would add a
`U`-edge at one of the saturated labels. These five co-points are distinct
actual graph neighbors of the original point represented by `P`, each with
`H`-degree four. Their contribution twenty contradicts (1), whose value for
this all-`q=2` size-three point is twelve.

If a size-three point remained, every one would have mode `(2,2,2)`. Make a
graph `R` whose vertices are the size-three points and whose edges are the
active labels at which two of them meet. It is simple, cubic, and
triangle-free. Write `e=|E(R)|`. Its `e` edge labels are ordinary `t=2`
labels; among the twelve ordinary active labels, the other `12-e` have
`t=0`.

At an edge label `i`, the third point is `{i,a_i}`. Singleton crossings force
`a_i` to have as `U`-neighbors the four other edge labels incident with the
two endpoints of edge `i` in `R`. Thus `a_i` cannot be special, and (5)
forces

```text
t_(a_i)=0,
N_U(a_i)=N_Line(R)(i).                                      (9)
```

The `e` distinct points `{i,a_i}` consume `e` of the
`3(12-e)` point incidences available at ordinary `t=0` labels. Therefore

```text
e <= 3(12-e),
e <= 9.                                                     (10)
```

A nonempty simple cubic triangle-free graph with at most nine edges has order
four or six. The order-four graph is `K4`, so the only possibility is
`R=K3,3`, with nine edges. Only three ordinary `t=0` labels remain. But the
open neighborhoods of the nine vertices of `Line(K3,3)`, the `3`-by-`3`
rook graph, are all different. Equation (9) consequently makes
`i -> a_i` injective, impossibly mapping nine edge labels into three labels.
There is no size-three point.

### The all-size-two residual is impossible

The 42 point incidences now form 21 size-two points, the edges of a simple
cubic triangle-free graph `F` on the fourteen labels. For every length-two
path `x-a-y` in `F`, the singleton crossing at `a` forces `xy` into `K`.

Take a special label `x`. It has three `F`-neighbors. At any one neighbor
`a`, the two other `F`-neighbors of `a` are distinct and, by
triangle-freeness, lie outside `N_F(x)`. Hence `x` has at least

```text
3+2=5
```

forced `K`-neighbors, contrary to its degree four. This excludes the mixed
`r=14` profile.

## The all-`q=2`, `r=15` profile

It remains to treat

```text
q(T)=2 for all 15 active labels,
K is 8-regular.                                             (11)
```

For every active point `P`, (1) becomes

```text
sum_{v:uv in E(G)} d_H(uv)=4|P|.                            (12)
```

### Excluding point sizes five and four

Flower expansion gives `|P|<=5`. If `|P|=5`, its ten petals have
pairwise-disjoint nonempty external parts among exactly ten outside labels.
Every petal is therefore size two. Its endpoint is `K`-adjacent to all of
`P`, giving each label of `P` at least `4+10=14` neighbors, contrary to
(11).

Suppose `|P|=4`. Its eight petals lie in eleven outside labels. If `h` petals
are size two, then

```text
11 >= h+2(8-h)=16-h,
h>=5.                                                       (13)
```

Their `h` endpoints are each adjacent to all of `P`. If `h>=6`, every root
already has at least `3+h>8` neighbors. If `h=5`, one of the remaining three
petals has an external part of size at least two at its root, disjoint from
the five common endpoints. That root has at least `3+5+2=10` neighbors.
Thus every point has size two or three.

### Exact local modes at a size-three point

Again let `t_i` count the size-three points through label `i`, and use
`F,U` as above. Now

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=5-t_i.                                               (14)
```

Fix a size-three point `P={1,2,3}`. At root `i`, let `a_i` count the other
size-three points whose `2`-by-`2` crossing with `P` is empty in `L`.
Such a crossing is either empty or the full `K2,2`. An empty crossing forces
four `U`-edges; a full crossing gives an actual graph edge of `H`-degree four.

At label `i`, petals based at the other two roots force at least

```text
sum_(j != i) (3-t_j+2a_j)
```

different `U`-neighbors. If

```text
f=sum_i (t_i-1-a_i)
```

is the number of full crossings, (12) gives `4f<=12`. Exact enumeration of
`t_i in {1,2,3}` and `0<=a_i<=t_i-1` under these inequalities leaves:

| sorted `t` | aligned `a` | forced `U` degrees | capacities | full crossings |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| `(1,1,1)` | `(0,0,0)` | `(4,4,4)` | `(4,4,4)` | 0 |
| `(1,2,2)` | `(0,0,0)` | `(2,3,3)` | `(4,3,3)` | 2 |
| `(2,2,2)` | `(0,0,0)` | `(2,2,2)` | `(3,3,3)` | 3 |
| `(2,2,3)` | `(0,0,1)` | `(3,3,2)` | `(3,3,2)` | 3 |

For a short hand check, put `T=sum t_i` and `A=sum a_i`. Summing the three
`U` inequalities gives `T>=3+4A`, while `f<=3` gives `A>=T-6`.
The remaining individual inequalities produce exactly the four rows above.
The standard-library checker enumerates the same finite set.

### A parity obstruction removes `t=3`

If a label `x` had `t_x=3`, exactly three size-three points would contain
it. The last table says that each of those points has mode `223` and exactly
one of its two crossings with the other points at `x` is empty.

Make a graph on those three point objects, joining a pair when their crossing
at `x` is empty. Every vertex would have degree one. A three-vertex graph
cannot be 1-regular. Therefore no label has `t=3`, and every size-three point
has mode

```text
111, 122, or 222.                                           (15)
```

Every crossing between two size-three points that meet is full in `L`.

### Labeled-crossing saturation removes mode `111`

Suppose `P={i,j,k}` has mode `111`. Its six other point sets are size-two
petals with six distinct endpoints; call their set `A`.

Every endpoint in `A` is `K`-adjacent to all three labels of `P`. Hence each
label of `P` already has exactly its eight `K`-neighbors:

```text
N_K(i)=(P-{i}) union A.                                     (16)
```

Let `Z` be the six remaining active labels. Complementarity gives every
`z in Z` three `L`-neighbors in `P`.

Let `u` be the original graph vertex represented by `P`, and consider any
graph neighbor `v`.

- If `S_v` meets `P`, it is one of the six size-two petals, and its crossing
  with `P` is empty.
- If `S_v` is disjoint from `P` and contains a label `z in Z`, the labeled
  crossing gives the copy of `z` degree three into `P`. This is impossible
  for an actual graph edge, since every non-overlap crossing degree must be
  zero or two.
- If `S_v` is disjoint from `P` and contained in `A`, its crossing with `P`
  is empty by (16).
- If `S_v` is inactive, its crossing is empty.

Thus every graph edge incident with `u` has `H`-degree zero. This contradicts
(12), whose value is twelve. Mode `111` is impossible.

### The final `H`-degree parity contradiction

Every size-three point now has mode `122` or `222`.

- A `122` point has two distinct meeting size-three co-points. Their crossings
  are full, contributing `4+4=8` to its fixed sum twelve. Every further
  positive incident `H`-degree must consequently be exactly four.
- A `222` point has three such co-points, contributing `12`; it has no other
  positive incident `H`-degree.
- If one endpoint point has size two, a positive degree-zero-or-two labeled
  crossing is necessarily a `K2,2`, hence has exactly four edges.
- An inactive endpoint contributes zero.

The only other positive crossing allowed by the degree-zero-or-two rule would
be a six-cycle between two disjoint size-three points. The first two bullets
show that neither a `122` nor a `222` point has capacity for degree six.
Therefore every graph edge has

```text
d_H(e) in {0,4}.                                            (17)
```

But the handshake identity in `H` gives

```text
sum_e d_H(e)=2|E(H)|=2n3=90,
```

which is not divisible by four. This contradiction excludes the last active
profile and hence excludes `n3=45`.
