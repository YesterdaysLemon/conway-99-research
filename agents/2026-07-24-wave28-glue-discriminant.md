## Result and status wall

Let

```text
L=(Z^44,S),             A_L=L*/L,
G=21S^-1,
h=det(S).
```

Every unrestricted endpoint lattice in the frozen Wave 28 package must
satisfy all of the following.

1. Its discriminant group and exact level are

   ```text
   A_L = (Z/3Z)^u direct_sum (Z/7Z)^v,
   (u,v)=(v_3(h),v_7(h)),
   level(L)=3^[u>0] 7^[v>0].
   ```

2. With the convention

   ```text
   q_p = direct_sum_i <2a_i/p>,
   delta_p = Legendre(product_i a_i,p),
   ```

   its discriminant form is one of exactly **twelve formal candidates**
   surviving the elementary-group and signature-44 Milgram conditions.

3. The scaled dual `G` has the same local determinant signs

   ```text
   delta_3(G)=delta_3(L),
   delta_7(G)=delta_7(L),
   ```

   while its elementary primary dimensions are complementary to those
   of `L`.

4. Any root `r` of norm two has divisibility one but does **not** split:

   ```text
   [L : (Zr direct_sum (r_perp intersect L))]=2.
   ```

   Its rank-43 complement has determinant `2h`, 2-primary discriminant
   form `<-1/2>`, and the same odd discriminant form as `L`.

5. The exact projector image `t=XSr` of any root lies in a finite
   alphabet code:

   ```text
   t in {0,+/-1,+/-2}^231,
   sum_i t_i=0,
   ||t||^2=42,
   t(r).t(s)=21(r,s)_S.
   ```

   The global cubic norm reduces its 46 possible coordinate-count
   patterns to 32, but a hostile pattern survives.

6. If `R` is generated by all roots, `Rbar` is its primitive closure,
   and `K=Rbar_perp intersect L`, then `R` is ADE, `K` is rootless, and
   `L` is controlled by two explicit isotropic glues. For every prime
   `p` other than 3 or 7,


## 2. Elementary discriminant group and exact level

Because `21S^-1` is integral,

```text
21L* is contained in L.
```

Thus `21A_L=0`. Since `|A_L|=det(S)=3^u7^v`, the primary decomposition is
necessarily

```text
A_L = (Z/3Z)^u direct_sum (Z/7Z)^v.              (1)
```

There can be no cyclic factor of order 9, 49, or 21: every invariant
factor is killed by 21.

For an even lattice, the level is the exponent of its finite quadratic
form. A nonzero elementary odd-primary component has a diagonal
presentation `<2a/p>` and exact level `p`. Every determinant here is
greater than one, so (1) gives

| `h` | `(u,v)` | exact level of `S` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | (2,0) | 3 |
| 21 | (1,1) | 21 |
| 49 | (0,2) | 7 |
| 81 | (4,0) | 3 |
| 189 | (3,1) | 21 |
| 441 | (2,2) | 21 |
| 729 | (6,0) | 3 |
| 1029 | (1,3) | 21 |

This is an exact-level statement only. It supplies no Atkin--Lehner
isometry and does not imply that `L` is strongly modular.

The same argument applied to `G`, using `21G^-1=S`, gives

```text
A_G =
  (Z/3Z)^(44-u) direct_sum (Z/7Z)^(44-v).         (2)
```

Equivalently,

```text
rank_Fp(S mod p)+rank_Fp(G mod p)=44
```

for `p=3,7`, with respective ranks `(44-u,u)` and `(44-v,v)`.

## 3. The twelve formal discriminant-form candidates

For `p=3,7`, diagonalize the nondegenerate elementary component as

```text
q_p(x)=sum_i 2a_i x_i^2/p  in Q/2Z.
```

Write

```text
delta_p=(product_i a_i / p) in {+1,-1},
```

where the empty component has `delta_p=+1`. Nondegenerate quadratic
spaces over `F_p`, for odd `p`, are classified by their dimension and
determinant square class, so these are all local isometry invariants
needed here.

Both 3 and 7 are `3 mod 4`. The normalized one-dimensional Gauss sum of
`<2a/p>` is

```text
i (a/p).
```

The standard Milgram identity for an even lattice of signature 44 is

```text
|A_L|^(-1/2) sum_(x in A_L) exp(pi i q_L(x))
  = exp(2 pi i * 44/8)
  = -1.                                           (3)
```

Consequently

```text
i^(u+v) delta_3 delta_7=-1,
delta_3 delta_7=(-1)^((u+v)/2+1).                 (4)
```

Every listed determinant is `1 mod 4`, so `u+v` is even as required.
Taking `2` as a canonical nonsquare modulo 3 and `3` as a canonical
nonsquare modulo 7 yields the complete formal census:

| `h` | `(u,v)` | required `delta_3 delta_7` | allowed `(delta_3,delta_7)` | canonical coefficient lists `(a_3;a_7)` |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 9 | (2,0) | + | `(+,+)` | `(1,1; empty)` |
| 21 | (1,1) | + | `(+,+)`, `(-,-)` | `(1;1)`, `(2;3)` |
| 49 | (0,2) | + | `(+,+)` | `(empty;1,1)` |
| 81 | (4,0) | - | `(-,+)` | `(1,1,1,2; empty)` |
| 189 | (3,1) | - | `(+,-)`, `(-,+)` | `(1,1,1;3)`, `(1,1,2;1)` |
| 441 | (2,2) | - | `(+,-)`, `(-,+)` | `(1,1;1,3)`, `(1,2;1,1)` |
| 729 | (6,0) | + | `(+,+)` | `(1,1,1,1,1,1; empty)` |
| 1029 | (1,3) | - | `(+,-)`, `(-,+)` | `(1;1,1,3)`, `(2;1,1,1)` |

There are four pure-prime rows with one type each and four mixed-prime
rows with two types each:

```text
4*1+4*2=12.                                      (5)
```

An earlier live integration snapshot incorrectly reported eleven. The
mistake is retained in `failed-routes.md` and the JSON correction record;
a hostile regression test now explicitly rejects eleven. Equation (5)
is the current claim.

The word *formal* matters: (3)--(5) classify the finite quadratic
modules not removed by the inherited group data and Milgram phase. They
do not prove positive-definite rank-44 realization, let alone projector
or Schur origin.

## 4. The scaled dual preserves both determinant signs

Fix `p` in `{3,7}`, put

```text
m=v_p(h),        ell=21/p,        n=44.
```

Because the `p`-part of `A_L` is elementary, a `Z_p` Jordan
decomposition has the form

```text
S is congruent to U orthogonal_sum pV,
rank(U)=n-m,
rank(V)=m,                                       (6)
```

with `U,V` unimodular. Then

```text
G=p ell S^-1
  is congruent to p ell U^-1 orthogonal_sum ell V^-1.  (7)
```

The discriminant component of `S` comes from `pV`; that of `G` comes
from `p ell U^-1`. In the convention of Section 3, determinant square
classes give

```text
delta_p(G)/delta_p(S)
 = (2/p)^n (ell/p)^(n-m)
   (det(U)det(V)/p).                              (8)
```

Modulo `p`-adic squares,

```text
det(U)det(V)=h/p^m.
```

For `p=3`, `(7/3)=+1` and `(2/3)^44=1`, so (8) is one. For `p=7`,
`(2/7)=+1`, `(3/7)=-1`, and its remaining exponent is

```text
44-v+u,
```

which is even because `u+v` is even. Therefore

```text
delta_3(G)=delta_3(S),
delta_7(G)=delta_7(S).                            (9)
```

The checker evaluates (8) for every determinant row and every candidate
in the twelve-form census.

## 5. A root is primitive but never an orthogonal `A1` summand

Let `r in L` satisfy `(r,r)=2`.

First, `r` is primitive. If `r=ka` with `k>=2`, then the positive even
integer `(a,a)=2/k^2` is impossible.

Let

```text
div(r)=gcd{(r,x): x in L}.
```

Since `(r,r)=2`, its divisibility is 1 or 2. If it were 2, then
`r/2 in L*` would define a 2-torsion element of `A_L`. But `|A_L|=h`
is odd, so `r/2 in L`, contradicting primitivity. Hence

```text
div(r)=1.                                        (10)
```

Put

```text
K_r=r_perp intersect L.
```

The map

```text
L -> Z/2Z,       x -> (r,x) mod 2
```

is surjective by (10). Its kernel is exactly `Zr direct_sum K_r`:
when `(r,x)` is even,

```text
x-((r,x)/2)r in K_r.
```

Therefore

```text
[L : Zr direct_sum K_r]=2.                       (11)
```

The determinant-index formula gives

```text
2 det(K_r)=4h,
det(K_r)=2h.                                     (12)
```

Since the overlattice in (11) has odd determinant, the 2-primary part of
`A_{K_r}` has order two. The nonzero glue element must cancel the
`A1` class `r/2`, whose norm is `+1/2`. Thus

```text
(A_{K_r},q_{K_r})_2=<-1/2>.                      (13)
```

The index is a power of two, so odd primary components are untouched:

```text
(A_{K_r},q_{K_r})_odd is isometric to (A_L,q_L). (14)
```

Equations (11)--(14) are the essential distinction between a primitive
root sublattice and an orthogonal integral summand. Calling the root
primitive and then splitting it would erase the required index-two glue.

## 6. Every root has a constrained 231-coordinate projector image

Let the rows of `X` be `x_i` and define

```text
t=t(r)=XSr in Z^231,
t_i=(x_i,r)_S.                                   (15)
```

The frame identity gives

```text
||t||^2
 =r^T S X^T X S r
 =21 r^T S r
 =42.                                            (16)
```

From `M1=0`, full column rank of `X`, and invertibility of `S`,

```text
X^T 1=0,
sum_i t_i=0.                                     (17)
```

Finally, `M_ii=(x_i,x_i)_S=4`. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

```text
|t_i| <= sqrt(4*2)<3.
```

Because `t_i` is integral,

```text
t_i in {0,+/-1,+/-2}.                            (18)
```

More generally, for any two roots,

```text
t(r).t(s)=21(r,s)_S.                             (19)
```

Thus the entire root system, whether or not primitive or orthogonally
split, must admit this exact integral 231-coordinate image.

Let `(a,b,z,d,e)` count entries `(+2,+1,0,-1,-2)`. Solving the length,
zero-sum, and squared-norm equations gives

```text
b=21-3a-e,
d=21-a-3e,
z=189+3(a+e).                                    (20)
```

Nonnegativity leaves exactly 46 pairs `(a,e)`.

There is one further inherited scalar constraint. Put

```text
y_i=S^(1/2)x_i,
T=sum_i y_i tensor y_i tensor y_i.
```

The Schur identity gives

```text
||T||^2
 =sum_(i,j) M_ij^3
 =tr(SQ)
 =60.                                            (21)
```

Projecting all three tensor legs onto the unit root direction
`S^(1/2)r/sqrt(2)` contributes squared energy

```text
(sum_i t_i^3)^2/8
 =9(a-e)^2/2.                                    (22)
```

Hence

```text
|a-e|<=3.                                        (23)
```

This removes 14 of the 46 count patterns and leaves exactly 32. The JSON
lists all 32. The filter is active: the moment-compatible mutation

```text
(a,b,z,d,e)=(4,9,201,17,0)
```

has pure-line cubic energy 72 and is rejected by (21). But the hostile
control

```text
(a,b,z,d,e)=(0,21,189,21,0)
```

has cubic energy zero and survives. Therefore the scalar root moments do
not exclude roots.

## 7. The full primitive root-closure and glue dictionary

Let `R` be the sublattice generated by **all** norm-two vectors of `L`.
The usual root-string argument for an integral positive-definite lattice
shows that its roots form a simply-laced finite root system. Thus

```text
R is an orthogonal sum of ADE root lattices.      (24)
```

This is an abstract description of `R`; it does not assert that `R` is an
orthogonal summand of `L`.

Define

```text
Rbar=(R tensor Q) intersect L,
H0=Rbar/R.                                       (25)
```

Then `Rbar` is primitive in `L`, and evenness makes `H0` an isotropic
subgroup of the ADE discriminant form `A_R`. Every nonzero coset used by
`H0` has minimum at least four. Indeed, a norm-two representative would
be another root of `L`, hence would already belong to `R`, contradicting
that the coset is nonzero.

Now put

```text
K=Rbar_perp intersect L.                         (26)
```

The complement `K` is primitive and rootless. A norm-two vector in `K`
would be a root of `L`, hence an element of `R`, while also being
orthogonal to `R`; positive definiteness then gives a contradiction.

Finally,

```text
H1=L/(Rbar direct_sum K)                         (27)
```

is isotropic in `A_Rbar direct_sum A_K`. Because both orthogonal
sublattices are primitive, the two projections of `H1` are injective.
Consequently `H1` is the graph of an anti-isometry between discriminant
subgroups. Standard overlattice bookkeeping gives

```text
q_L is isometric to H1_perp/H1,                  (28)
h=det(R)det(K)/(|H0|^2 |H1|^2).                 (29)
```

By Section 3, the residual form in (28) is one of the twelve formal
candidates.

There is a useful full-cancellation condition away from 21. If
`p` is not 3 or 7, then `(A_L)_p=0`, so

```text
(H1)_p=(H1)_p^perp.
```

Together with injectivity of both projections, the order identity

```text
|(H1)_p|^2=|(A_Rbar)_p| |(A_K)_p|
```

forces both projections to be surjective. Therefore

```text
(A_K,q_K)_p is isometric to
(A_Rbar,-q_Rbar)_p,                              (30)
v_p(det K)=v_p(det Rbar).                        (31)
```

For `p=2`, (30) specializes to the cancellation in (13). More generally,
every prime-to-21 discriminant factor contributed by the primitive root
closure must be duplicated with the opposite form in the rootless
complement and completely consumed by glue.

## 8. What is now finite, and what remains open

The reduction splits the remaining root question into two cases.

If `rank(R)=44`, then `Rbar=L` and `K=0`. The problem is finite in
principle:

```text
enumerate rank-44 ADE root systems R;
enumerate isotropic H0 in A_R;
retain only det(R)/|H0|^2 in the eight h rows;
reject every nonzero H0 coset of minimum two;
match the residual form to the twelve candidates;
then impose the 231-coordinate code and Schur origin.
```

This is a root-preserving even-overlattice classification, not the
orthogonal ADE census already completed in Wave 27.

If `rank(R)<44`, the rootless positive-definite complement `K` has
positive rank. Equations (28)--(31) are exact, but the class of such
rootless complements is not yet finite under the present invariants.
Determinant-only ADE enumeration cannot solve this case: it neither
classifies `K` nor checks isotropic glue, absence of new roots, the
projector code (15)--(23), or Schur origin.

This is the current honest blocker:

```text
classify root-preserving H0 and compatible rootless K/H1,
or derive a new tensor/vector-valued modular constraint that couples
the twelve residual forms to the 231-row projector realization.
```
