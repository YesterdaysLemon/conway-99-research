## 1. Catalog scope and validation

The frozen structural reduction leaves exactly:

```text
455 connected cubic girth>=5 graphs of order 18;
2 disjoint unions K3,3 + connected cubic girth>=5 graph of order 12.
```

The two official compressed catalogs are fetched by HTTPS into memory.  Both
compressed byte streams are checked for exact length and SHA-256 before
decompression.  The decompressed streams are again checked for exact length
and SHA-256.  The parser then requires exactly 455 and 2 distinct nonempty
short graph6 records.

Every decoded record is independently checked for:

- the declared order;
- exactly `3n/2` simple edges;
- degree three at every vertex;
- connectivity;
- absence of triangles; and
- absence of 4-cycles.

The two order-12 records are relabeled onto vertices `6,...,17` and combined
with a fixed `K3,3` on `0,...,5`.  Catalog bytes and graph6 strings never
appear in an output artifact.  Cases are identified by catalog order, index,
and the SHA-256 of the individual record.

Completeness and nonisomorphism of the official catalogs remain external
premises.  The checker verifies the supplied bytes and their semantics, not
the House of Graphs generation theorem or licensing.

## 2. Fixed-F exact residual

Fix one point graph `F`.  Its 27 edges are the active original vertices.
Let

```text
K_0 = E(F) union {pairs of F-neighbors}.
```

The frozen point-clique and meeting-crossing rules put every pair in `K_0`
inside `K`, hence outside `L`.

A compatible support candidate is a pair of disjoint `F`-edges whose four
endpoint pairs all avoid `K_0`.  A residual 2-factor `R` may use only these
candidates.  If `y_s` is the binary selection variable for candidate `s`,
the exact conditions are:

```text
sum_{s incident with point p} y_s = 2                 for every p in E(F),

sum_{s whose rectangle covers label pair ab} y_s
  is either 0 or 2                                    for every ab.
```

The second line is the coordinate form of

```text
N A_R N^T = 2 A_L.
```

The raw PySAT encoding uses exact point-degree cardinalities, an at-most-two
coverage constraint, and clauses excluding a singleton cover.  It returned
`UNSAT` for all 457 cases.  Since no DRAT/LRAT/PB proof was generated and
checked, the frozen raw status is only:

```text
457 UNSAT_UNVERIFIED at exact coverage;
matching/common-cap/spectral/binary stages NOT_REACHED.
```

The exact certificate below replaces those raw statuses as the reason for
the derived result.

## 3. Connected cases: binary kernel certificate

Reduce the fixed-F equations modulo two.  Let `m` be the number of compatible
support candidates and form the `180`-by-`m` binary matrix `M` with:

```text
27 point-incidence rows;
153 rectangle-coverage rows.
```

Every exact residual selection vector `y` must satisfy

```text
M y = 0 over F_2.                                    (1)
```

The point rows are even because the required degree is two.  The rectangle
rows are even because every coverage count is zero or two.

For each connected catalog record, deterministic reduced row echelon form
produces the exact rank and a canonical nullspace basis.  The certificate
stores SHA-256 hashes of:

- the ordered parity matrix rows;
- the nonzero reduced rows; and
- the ordered nullspace basis.

The nullity distribution is:

| nullity | cases | kernel size per case |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 443 | 1 |
| 1 | 4 | 2 |
| 2 | 5 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 | 16 |
| 18 | 1 | 262,144 |

For each basis, the verifier enumerates the full kernel in Gray-code order.
For every vector it computes all 27 **integer**, not merely parity, point
degrees.  No enumerated vector has degree exactly two at every point:

```text
total connected cases:                 455
maximum kernel vectors in one case:    262144
vectors passing all point degrees:     0
```

Any exact `R` would satisfy (1) and the integer point degrees, so this is a
complete obstruction.  Checking the stronger integer rectangle counts is
unnecessary after no vector survives the point-degree test.

This is not an inference from a solver exit code.  The certificate specifies
the input case, matrix and reduction hashes, nullity, exhaustive vector count,
and zero survivor count.  The standalone verifier reconstructs each item
from the official inputs.

## 4. Mixed cases: human-checkable count

In a residual

```text
F=K3,3 disjoint union F_12,
```

the point vertices split into the nine edges of `K3,3` and the eighteen
edges of `F_12`.

Every pair of labels inside the `K3,3` component lies in `K`: opposite-side
pairs are `F`-edges and same-side pairs have three common `F`-neighbors.
Therefore no compatible support candidate is internal to the nine-point
side.

The exact candidate counts are:

| order-12 catalog index | all candidates | cross candidates | internal `K3,3` | internal order-12 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 163 | 162 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 162 | 162 | 0 | 0 |

In both rows all `9*18=162` cross point-pairs are compatible.

If a 2-factor `R` existed, the nine `K3,3` point vertices, with no internal
candidate, would force

```text
9*2=18
```

selected cross edges.  The eighteen order-12 point vertices need total
degree `18*2=36`.  The cross edges contribute 18, leaving degree sum 18,
which requires

```text
18/2=9
```

selected order-12-internal edges.  Only one or zero candidates exist.  This
contradiction excludes both mixed cases before any coverage, matching,
common-neighbor, or spectral condition is needed.

## 5. Independent semantic replay

- it imports neither `census.py` nor `build_certificate.py`;
- it uses only the Python standard library;
- it fetches and validates both official byte streams independently;
- it implements a separate graph6 parser and graph validator;
- it independently reconstructs `K_0` and every compatible support;
- it independently rebuilds all parity matrices, RREFs, bases, and kernel
  enumerations;
- it independently reconstructs the two mixed-component counts; and
- it compares the full JSON object, not a selected digest.

Its frozen result is:


The six focused tests check binary RREF/basis recovery, a negative kernel

## 6. Failed runs, repairs, and hostile boundary

1. The first 50-case scout exposed a `CardEnc.equals` exception when a point
   had fewer than two compatible candidates.  The encoding was repaired to
   add an explicit contradictory pair of unit clauses in this arithmetically
   impossible situation.  The final complete run has deterministic output.
2. The raw SAT result is deliberately not promoted.  No proof trace was
   checked, even though its 457 statuses agree with the exact certificate.
3. GF(2) rank alone does not reject every case.  Twelve connected kernels
   are nontrivial and the two mixed kernels have nullity 92.  Complete kernel
   enumeration handles the former; the direct integer count handles the
   latter.
4. The later independent-cross-edge matching, active common-neighbor caps,
   exact spectrum, and binary component/nullity stages were never needed.
   No outcome is claimed for them in isolation.
5. The standalone verifier is implementation-independent but was still
   written by the discovery lane.  Protocol-level `VERIFIED` status requires
   another agent to audit both the frozen structural reduction and this
   certificate.

The strongest objection is catalog provenance: the complete reduction
depends on the official files being complete nonisomorphic catalogs of the
declared graph classes.  Exact hashes pin the bytes, but no locally checked
generation proof is supplied.  The second objection is the imported
structural bridge itself, especially that every equality residual is
represented by precisely one of these 457 `F` types and that every support
edge lies among the defined compatible candidates.
