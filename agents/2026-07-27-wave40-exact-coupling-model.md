## Three-fibre geometry

Fix any edge `xy` and its unique triangle mate `z`. The sets

```text
X=N(x)-{y,z}, Y=N(y)-{x,z}, Z=N(z)-{x,y}
```

each have twelve vertices. Their internal relations and all three pairwise
relations are perfect matchings.

Fix the `X` matching and pull `Y` through the `X-Y` matching. The remaining
`Y` matching is one of all 10,395 perfect matchings on twelve points. Their
alternating components give exactly the eleven positive partitions of six.
For every one of the 10,395 choices, the checker explicitly constructs a
relabeling centralizing the fixed `X` matching and carrying it to the
retained representative of its partition. This is a complete local
relabeling argument, not an automorphism assumption on a completed graph.

Index `Z` through the `X-Z` perfect matching. Since `Y-Z` is also a perfect
matching, the twelve neighbour signatures have the form

```text
(i,f(i)), i=0,...,11
```

for an arbitrary permutation `f`.

## Rank coupling

`{x,y,z} union X union Y`, let `U_f` be the twelve third-fibre columns, and
let `K` be a kernel basis for `S`. An invertible congruence gives

```text
rank [ S       U_f ] >= rank(S) + 2 rank(K^T U_f).
     [ U_f^T   W   ]
```

for every possible third-fibre block `W`. After reducing `S` to
`diag(D,0)`, the residual matrix contains

```text
[ 0  U0 ]
[ U0^T W' ],
```

and `rank(U0)=rank(K^T U_f)`. Basis changes expose
`rank(U0)` nonsingular hyperbolic pairs, proving the inequality. Thus even an
unknown `W` cannot cancel the contribution.
