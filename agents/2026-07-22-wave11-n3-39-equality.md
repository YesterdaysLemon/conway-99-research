## Equality setup and explicit proof boundary

Assume that a putative `srg(99,14,1,2)` has exactly `n3=39` induced copies
of `N3`. Retain the Wave 6 graph `H` on graph edges and the Wave 7 graph `L`
on graph triangles. A triangle is *active* when `q(T)>0`, and `K` is the
simple complement of the subgraph of `L` induced by the active triangles.
This complement relation is an essential premise, not notation that may be
dropped.

For every original graph vertex `u`, let `S_u` be the active graph triangles
containing `u`. The previously checked framework gives:

1. `q(T)=0` or `q(T)>=2`, `d_L(T)=3q(T)`, and
   `sum_T q(T)=2n3/3`;
2. every active triangle belongs to exactly three point sets `S_u`;
3. active point sets have size at least two and are cliques in `K`;
4. the point hypergraph is linear;
5. three distinct point sets cannot pairwise meet at three distinct active
   triangles (the common-point or Berge-triangle rule); and
6. after deleting the common active triangle from two meeting point sets,
   every degree in their labeled bipartite `L`-crossing is zero or two.

No connectedness, vertex-transitivity, or completed-graph automorphism is
assumed.

Since `sum q=26` and `3q(T)<=r-1` on `r` active triangles, exact integer
enumeration leaves only

```text
r=13: (2^13),
r=12: (2^10,3^2),
r=11: (2^7,3^4),
r=10: (2^4,3^6).
```

The corresponding `K`-degree multisets are

```text
6^13,
5^10 2^2,
4^7 1^4,
3^4 0^6.
```

The three non-singleton point cliques through a triangle require three
distinct incident `K`-edges. The mixed profiles therefore force respectively
2, 8, and 18 singleton incidences, contradicting the Wave 9 singleton lemma.
Consequently

```text
r=13, q(T)=2 for every active T, and K is 6-regular.          (1)
```

## Expansion bounds every active point size by four

Fix an active point set `P` of size `s`. At each `i` in `P`, the other two
point sets through `i` each contribute at least one representative outside
`P`. The resulting `2s` representatives are pairwise distinct:

- a collision between the two sets based at one `i` violates linearity; and
- a collision between sets based at distinct `i,j` makes those two sets and
  `P` pairwise meet at the three distinct triangles `i,j,x`, violating the
  common-point rule.

There are only `13-s` active triangles outside `P`, hence

```text
2s <= 13-s, so s <= 4.                                      (2)
```

## Size four is impossible

Let `|P|=4`. Its four occurrences have eight other point sets, or *petals*,
whose external parts are pairwise disjoint and lie among the nine active
triangles outside `P`. A size-four petal would use three external places and
leave at least seven for the remaining petals, exceeding nine. Two size-three
petals would use `2+2` places and leave at least six, again exceeding nine.
Thus no petal has size four and at most one has size three. At least three of
the four occurrences are consequently of type `224`.

At a `224` occurrence `i`, each size-two petal has a singleton external side.
Its crossing with `P-{i}` must be empty: a nonempty singleton-side crossing
would have degree one on the other side. Because `K` is the complement of
`L`, each of the two endpoints is `K`-adjacent to all three vertices of
`P-{i}` and, by its point clique, to `i` as well.

Choose two `224` occurrences. Linearity and the common-point rule make their
four endpoints distinct. Every vertex of `P` now has its three internal
neighbors plus all four endpoints as `K`-neighbors, for degree at least seven.
This contradicts (1). Therefore

```text
every active point set has size two or three.                 (3)
```

## The `F/U` degree table

Let `F` be the union of the point-clique edges in `K`, and put
`U=K-E(F)`. If `t_i` of the three point sets through active triangle `i`
have size three, then linearity makes their incident `F`-edges distinct and

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=3-t_i.                                                (4)
```

In particular every active triangle has `U`-degree at most three.

## A `233` occurrence is impossible

At a `233` occurrence `i`, write the size-two point as `{i,a}` and the two
size-three points as `{i,j,k}` and `{i,l,m}`. Each crossing having singleton
side `{a}` is empty, so complementarity forces

```text
aj, ak, al, am in E(K).                                      (5)
```

None of these four edges can lie in `F`. For example, a point set containing
`a,j`, together with `{i,a}` and `{i,j,k}`, would have pairwise intersections
at the three distinct triangles `a,j,i`; the other three cases are identical.
Thus all four edges in (5) lie in `U`, contradicting `d_U(a)<=3` from (4).
There are no `233` occurrences.

## A size-three point is impossible

Let `P={i,j,k}` have size three. It cannot have three `333` occurrences:
the six other size-three point sets would contribute twelve pairwise-distinct
external active triangles, but only ten lie outside `P`. Since `233` has
already been excluded, `P` has a `223` occurrence, say at `i`, with size-two
points `{i,a}` and `{i,b}`.

All three singleton-side crossings are empty. Complementarity forces

```text
aj, ak, bj, bk, ab in E(K).                                  (6)
```

The common-point rule excludes an `F`-owner for every edge in (6), so they
all lie in `U`. Vertices `j` and `k` therefore each have at least two
`U`-neighbors. They already lie in the size-three point `P`, so (4) gives
`d_U(j),d_U(k)<=2`. Equality follows, `t_j=t_k=1`, and the occurrences of
`P` at both `j` and `k` must themselves be `223`.

Let the two endpoints at `j` be `c,d` and those at `k` be `e,f`. The same
linearity/common-point collision argument used in (2) makes all six endpoints
`a,b,c,d,e,f` distinct. Repeating (6) at `j` and `k` forces

```text
ic, id, ie, if in E(U).
```

But the original `223` occurrence has `t_i=1`, hence `d_U(i)=2`, a
contradiction. No active point set has size three.

## Final parity contradiction

Only size-two point sets remain. The total point incidence is

```text
13 active triangles * 3 point sets per triangle = 39.
```

It cannot be the sum of the even sizes of size-two point sets. This excludes
`n3=39`.

Since `n3` is a multiple of three and Wave 10 already gives `n3>=39`, the
conditional consequences are

```text
n3 >= 42,
induced_C6_count = 209286+n3 >= 209328,
branch bounds = 42,42,42,48,48,42,42,48,42,48,42,48.
```

## Failed checks and repairs retained in the record

The first submitted exposition used empty `L`-crossings to infer `K`-edges
without restating that `K` is the simple complement of `L` on distinct active
triangles. An adversarial verifier built a diagnostic 13-vertex countermodel
for the weakened formulation in which `K` and `L` were independent. That did
not refute the intended Wave 7 definition, but it did refute the written proof.
The proof boundary above now makes complementarity and the point-clique
premise explicit at every use.

The first compact checker was also rejected as a faithful audit artifact. It
classified capacity before local type, reporting `7/50/7`; six of those seven
capacity profiles also contain a `233` occurrence. Its tests could not detect
mutating a justified coefficient two to an unsupported coefficient three, and
its common-point helper was not on the production path. The repaired checker
reports the raw proof partition

```text
56 profiles containing 233,
1 all-333 capacity profile,
7 other homogeneous 223/333 profiles,
```

records the six-profile overlap separately, and constructs literal forced-edge
and forbidden-owner sets. These failures and repairs are detailed in the
independent audit rather than erased.
