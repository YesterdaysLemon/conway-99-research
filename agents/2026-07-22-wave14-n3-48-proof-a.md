conditional premises are used.

   ```text

   ```

2. `L` has graph-triangles as vertices and induced-`N3` side pairs as edges.
   For an active triangle `T`,

   ```text
   q(T)>=2,
   d_L(T)=3q(T),
   sum_T q(T)=2n3/3=32.
   ```

3. On the `r` active labels, `K` is the simple complement of `L`.
4. For every original graph vertex `u`, its nonempty active point
   `S_u` is a clique in `K` and has size at least two.  The indexed point
   family is linear, every active label lies in exactly three points, and
   three points cannot meet pairwise at three distinct labels.
5. If actual graph edge `uv` joins points `P=S_u,Q=S_v`, delete both labeled
   copies of their possible common active label.  Every remaining row and
   column of the bipartite `L`-crossing has degree zero or two, and

   ```text
   d_H(uv)=e_L(P,Q),
   sum_{v:uv in E(G)} d_H(uv)=2 sum_{T in P}q(T).       (1)
   ```

The words *actual graph edge*, *labeled*, and *both row and column* are
essential.  No crossing assertion is made for an arbitrary pair of original

## 3. Lemma 2 — the mixed `r=14` profile is impossible

Assume

```text
q=(2^10,3^4), d_K=(7^10,4^4).
```

Call `q=2` labels ordinary and `q=3` labels special.

For a point `P` of size `s`, the two other points through each label of `P`
have pairwise-disjoint nonempty external parts.  Hence

```text
2s<=14-s,
s<=4.                                                     (2)
```

If `s=4`, its eight petals lie in ten external labels, so at least six have
singleton external part.  Their six distinct endpoints are `K`-adjacent to
all four roots.  Every root would have at least `3+6=9` `K`-neighbors,
above the maximum seven.  Thus all points have size two or three.

Let `t_i` be the number of size-three points through label `i`, let `F` be
the union of all point-clique edges, and put `U=K-E(F)`.  Linearity gives

```text
d_F(i)=3+t_i,
d_U(i)=4-t_i  for an ordinary label,
d_U(i)=1-t_i  for a special label.                        (3)
```

A size-three point cannot contain a special label `x`.  Such a point has
`t_x=1,d_U(x)=0`.  Singleton petals at the other two roots force both of
their `t` values to be three.  The two singleton petals at `x` then force
two distinct `U`-neighbors at either ordinary root, whose `U`-degree is only
one.

Now let ordinary size-three point `P={i,j,k}` remain.  Singleton petals and
external-label capacity give

```text
4-t_i >= (3-t_j)+(3-t_k)  cyclically,
t_i+t_j+t_k<=8.                                           (4)
```

The only sorted triples are `(2,2,2)` and `(2,3,3)`.  In the second mode,
the singleton petal at the `t=2` root saturates one `U`-edge at each
`t=3` root.  The five other size-three co-points of `P` must consequently
have full `2`-by-`2` `L`-crossing with `P`.  They are five distinct actual
graph neighbors and contribute `5*4=20` to (1), whose right side is twelve.
So only `(2,2,2)` is possible.

Make a graph `R_3` on size-three point objects, joining two at every label
with `t=2`.  It is simple, cubic, and triangle-free.  If `e=|E(R_3)|`,
its `e` edge labels are ordinary.  At such label `i`, the third point is
`{i,a_i}`.  Singleton forcing gives `a_i` four distinct `U`-neighbors, so
`a_i` is an ordinary `t=0` label.  The distinct points `{i,a_i}` consume
`e` incidences among the `3(10-e)` incidences at ordinary `t=0` labels:

```text
e<=3(10-e), hence e<=7.                                   (5)
```

A nonempty simple cubic triangle-free graph has at least six vertices and
nine edges.  This contradicts (5).  No size-three point exists.

The all-size-two point graph is now a simple cubic triangle-free graph on
the fourteen labels.  At a special label `x`, choose a length-two path
`x-a-y`.  Singleton crossing forces `xy in K`.  The two other neighbors of
`a` are distinct and lie outside `N_F(x)` by triangle-freeness.  Together
with the three `F`-neighbors of `x`, they give five `K`-neighbors at a label
of `K`-degree four.  Contradiction.

Therefore the mixed `r=14` profile is excluded.

## 4. Lemma 3 — the mixed `r=15` profile is impossible

Assume

```text
q=(2^13,3^2), d_K=(8^13,5^2).
```

Expansion gives point size at most five.  A size-five point has all ten
singleton petals, already giving each root at least `4+10` `K`-neighbors.  A
size-four point has at least five singleton petals.  Six or more exceed
degree eight directly; with exactly five, each of the three remaining petals
has two external labels and its base root has degree at least
`3+5+2=10`.  Thus every point has size two or three.

No size-three point contains a special label.  To see this, write
`t_i` as above.  Here

```text
d_U=5-t  on ordinary labels,
d_U=2-t  on special labels.                              (6)
```

If a point contains two special labels, adding the two forced-neighbor
inequalities at those roots gives `2t_ordinary>=8`, impossible because
`t_ordinary<=3`.

If it contains exactly one special root `x`, its other two roots `y,z` must
both have `t=3`.  For `t_x=1`, the two singleton petals at `x` saturate the
two `U`-edges at `y,z`; all four size-three co-point crossings based at
`y,z` are full.  For `t_x=2`, `d_U(x)=0`, so the same four crossings are
again full.  Either way they contribute sixteen to the fixed-point sum

```text
2(q_x+q_y+q_z)=2(3+2+2)=14,
```

which is impossible.  The exact local enumerator independently returns zero
states for both special-root patterns.

Each special label therefore lies in three size-two points.  By linearity at
most one of those points also contains the other special label, so choose a
special-ordinary size-two point `P`.  Its fixed-point sum is

```text
2(3+2)=10.                                                (7)
```

Every possible graph neighbor has active point size zero, two, or three.
After deleting an overlap, a two-sided `0/2` crossing with a size-two
endpoint has size zero or four; an inactive endpoint contributes zero.
Thus every term in (7) is divisible by four, but their sum is ten.
Contradiction.

Therefore the mixed `r=15` profile is excluded.

## 5. Lemma 4 — point sizes in the `r=16` profile

The only profile left is

```text
q=(2^16), d_K=(9^16).                                    (8)
```

Expansion gives point size at most five.  A size-five point has at least nine
singleton petals and is immediately impossible.

Let `P` have size four and let `h` of its eight petals have singleton
external part.  The twelve labels outside `P` give `h>=4`.

- If `h>=7`, the internal `K3` plus the common endpoints exceed degree nine.
- If `h=5` or `6`, a non-singleton petal adds two further point-clique
  neighbors at its base root, again exceeding nine.
- If `h=4`, external capacity is exact: the other four petals all have size
  three.  Avoiding immediate overdegree forces exactly one size-three petal
  at each root.  Every root is then saturated by the three internal labels,
  four singleton endpoints, and its own size-three petal pair.

In the last case, take the size-three petal based at root `i`.  Its two
external labels are outside every other root's saturated `K`-neighborhood.
Its crossing with `P-{i}` is therefore the full `K_(2,3)` in `L`, giving
degree three on both external labels.  This violates the inherited
row-and-column `0/2` law for their actual graph edge.

Hence every active point has size two or three.

## 7. Lemma 6 — the two zero-full-crossing modes are impossible

### Mode `111`

The six singleton-petal endpoints form a set `A`; every member of `A` is
`K`-adjacent to all of `P`.  Let `Z` be the other seven active labels.
Each root has exactly one unused `K`-edge into `Z`.  If `k_z` is the number
of roots `K`-adjacent to `z`, then

```text
sum_(z in Z) k_z=3.
```

A label of a disjoint actual-neighbor point must have `L`-degree zero or two
into `P`, so it needs `k_z=3` or `1`.  The three possible distributions are:

1. one column with `k=3`: every admissible crossing is empty;
2. columns `k=2,1`: the sole `k=1` column gives two `L`-edges of degree one
   on the `P` side, so it cannot occur in an actual-neighbor crossing; or
3. three `k=1` columns: row sums force their missing roots to be distinct.
   No one- or two-column subset has two-sided degrees in `{0,2}`.  The
   three-column subset gives one possible six-cycle crossing.

Linearity permits at most one point containing all three columns.  Thus the
total positive contribution at `P` is at most six, below its fixed-point sum
twelve.  Mode `111` is impossible.

### Mode `112` with its unique size-three crossing empty

The five singleton endpoints and the empty co-point's external pair give
seven labels `K`-adjacent to all of `P`.  They exactly fill every root's
seven external `K`-neighbors.  The remaining six labels are `L`-adjacent to
all three roots.

A disjoint point using one of the remaining labels has a crossing row of
degree three; a point using only the first seven labels has empty crossing.
Meeting petals and inactive points also contribute zero.  Every graph edge
at `P` would have `H`-degree zero, contradicting its fixed sum twelve.

The two `f=0` rows are excluded.  Eight local modes remain.

At a `t=2` label, symmetry of crossing emptiness says the two incident
size-three points either both record `a=0` or both record `a=1`; the latter
can occur only through the surviving `222`-empty mode.  At a `t=3` label,
the empty pairs form a graph on three point objects of maximum degree one,
so it is empty or one edge plus an isolate.  At least one incident point is
therefore a `123` point with its crossing at that label full.  These are
additional finite residual constraints, not contradictions.

## 8. Lemma 7 — exact finite support residual

- for `f>=2`, the remaining capacity is at most four.



```text

each L-edge has exact R-coverage two.                     (13)
```
