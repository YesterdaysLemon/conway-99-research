## Equality setup

Assume that a putative `srg(99,14,1,2)` has exactly `n3=36` induced copies
of `N3`. Retain the Wave 6 graph `H` on graph-edges, the Wave 7 graph `L` on
graph-triangles, the active-triangle quantities `q(T)`, and the Wave 9 labeled
crossing lemma. The established identities are

```text
q(T)=0 or q(T)>=2,
d_L(T)=3q(T),
sum_T q(T)=2n3/3=24,
d_H(uv) in {0,4,6,8,10,12},
sum_{v:uv in E(G)} d_H(uv)=2 sum_{T in S_u} q(T).
```

Here `S_u` is the set of active graph-triangles containing the original graph
vertex `u`. It is a clique in the active complement `K=overline(L)`. Distinct
point cliques through an active triangle consume disjoint incident `K`-edges.
The Wave 9 labeled crossing argument also gives

```text
no active point set S_u is a singleton.                       (1)
```

If there are `r` active triangles, exact integer enumeration under
`3q(T)<=r-1` leaves precisely

```text
r=10: (q(T))=(2^6,3^4),
r=11: (q(T))=(2^9,3^2),
r=12: (q(T))=(2^12).
```

No connectedness or completed-graph automorphism assumption is made.

## Excluding the mixed profiles

The `K`-degree of an active triangle is

```text
d_K(T)=r-1-3q(T).
```

Its three original points give three point cliques whose consumed incident
`K`-edges are distinct. Since every clique has size at least two by (1), every
active triangle needs at least three incident `K`-edges.

In the `r=10` profile, each `q=3` triangle has `K`-degree zero, forcing all
three of its points to be singletons. In the `r=11` profile, each `q=3`
triangle has `K`-degree one, so at least two of its points are singletons.
Both contradict (1). Thus only the all-`q=2` profile remains.

## Local point types and the common-point rule

Now `K` is 5-regular on twelve active triangles. At each active triangle, the
three non-singleton point cliques consume at most five incident edges. Their
only sorted size patterns are

```text
222, 223, 224, 233.                                          (2)
```

For two point sets meeting in an active triangle, delete the common isolated
labeled copy of that triangle. Every remaining vertex of their bipartite
`L`-crossing graph has degree zero or two. Directly, or by the crossing-size
bounds together with the allowed `H`-degrees, this gives

```text
2-by-2, 2-by-3, and 2-by-4 crossings: empty;
3-by-3 crossings: empty or a full K2,2.                       (3)
```

We also need a consequence of `lambda=1`.

**Common-point rule.** Three distinct active point sets cannot pairwise meet
at three distinct active triangles. This is the target specialization of the
regular-clique-assembly obstruction in Guest--Hammer--Johnson--Roblee,
Lemma 1; Petro--Phillips use the same clique-graph obstruction. The short
target-specific proof is included here to keep the dependency explicit.

Indeed, the corresponding three original vertices would be pairwise adjacent.
They therefore form their unique graph-triangle. Each of their three graph
edges would then lie both in that triangle and in the distinct active triangle
at which the two point sets met, contradicting `lambda=1`.

## A `K6` component is impossible

Suppose `K` contains a `K6`. Since `K` is 5-regular on twelve vertices, it is

```text
K = K6 disjoint-union K6.                                    (4)
```

Every point clique lies in one component. Across the two components, `L` is
complete bipartite. If an actual graph edge joins point sets of sizes `s,t`,
its labeled crossing is `K_(s,t)`: the local degrees are `t` and `s`. The
degree-zero-or-two rule permits a positive crossing only when `s=t=2`.
A point of size at least three would consequently have every incident
`H`-degree zero, contradicting its positive fixed-point sum. Hence all active
point sets have size two.

Within one `K6`, let `F` consist of the edges consumed by size-two point sets.
Every active triangle has three original points, so `F` is cubic on six
vertices and has nine edges. It is triangle-free: a triangle with edges
`ij,ik,jk` would make the three corresponding point objects pairwise meet at
the three distinct active triangles `i,j,k`, violating the common-point rule.
Mantel equality, or the exact six-vertex census, therefore gives

```text
F = K3,3.                                                     (5)
```

The nine original point objects in this component are indexed by the edges of
`F`. Two objects are adjacent when their `F`-edges meet, so the forced graph is
`L(K3,3)`, the `3`-by-`3` rook graph. This is the exact induced graph:

- two incident `F`-edges have the third edge at their shared endpoint as one
  common neighbor, exhausting `lambda=1`;
- two disjoint `F`-edges have the two cross-corner edges as two common
  neighbors, so they cannot be adjacent and they exhaust `mu=2`.

Thus every pair of vertices in the rook graph has already attained its full
SRG common-neighbor allowance. No outside original vertex can be adjacent to
two vertices of this rook graph.

On the other hand, a size-two point has fixed-point sum eight. Its only
positive `H`-edges go to size-two points in the other component and have
degree four, so it has exactly two such graph neighbors. It is therefore an
outside common neighbor of two vertices of the opposite rook graph, a
contradiction. This proves

```text
K has no K6 component.                                       (6)
```

## Eliminating point sizes four and three

A type `224` active triangle has a size-four point and two size-two points.
The zero-crossing alternatives in (3) make its five `K`-neighbors a clique,
producing a `K6`, contrary to (6).

At a type `233` triangle, the two external pairs belonging to the two
size-three points are either complete or anticomplete in `K`. The complete
case again produces a `K6`. Hence every surviving `233` occurrence is
**split**: the two external `K2` pairs are anticomplete in `K`.

Let a size-three point be

```text
C={i,j,k}.
```

Suppose first that it has a `223` occurrence at `i`, with size-two point sets
`{i,a}` and `{i,b}`. The zero crossings in (3) make

```text
{i,j,k,a,b}
```

a `K5`. The occurrence at `j` cannot be `233`: its other size-three point
would require two of the three available neighbors `{a,b,x}`, while splitness
requires both to be nonadjacent to `i,k`; only `x` can qualify. Therefore the
occurrence at `j` is also `223`.

Let its two size-two endpoints be `c,d`. The zero crossings force both to be
adjacent in `K` to `i`, so `c,d` lie in `{a,b,x}`. Neither may reuse `a` or
`b`. For example, reusing `a` makes

```text
C, {i,a}, {j,a}
```

pairwise meet at the three distinct active triangles `i,j,a`, violating the
common-point rule. Only `x` remains for the two distinct endpoints `c,d`, an
impossibility. Hence no size-three point has a `223` occurrence.

All occurrences of every size-three point must therefore be split `233`.
Build a graph `R` whose vertices are the size-three points and whose edges are
the active triangles at which two of them meet. Point-clique linearity makes
`R` simple, and every size-three point occurs three times, so `R` is cubic.

For an edge `i` of `R`, let `{i,m}` be its unique size-two point. The split
local structure makes `m` adjacent in `K` to all four line-graph neighbors of
`i`. Vertex `m` cannot be type `222`. If its other size-two endpoints are
`p,q`, the local `222` rule makes `p,q` adjacent in `K`. Splitness at `i`
then puts them in the same one of the two line-neighbor pairs. A size-three
point on that side, `{i,p,q}`, together with `{i,m}` and `{m,p}`, violates
the common-point rule.

Thus `m` is also type `233`, and the size-two points pair the edges of `R`.
For a matched pair `i,m`, the two edges are disjoint in `R`; otherwise their
`K`-edge would be consumed by both a size-three point and their size-two
point. The two split local neighborhoods give

```text
N_(L(R))(i)=N_(L(R))(m).                                     (7)
```

Write the disjoint edges as `i=uv` and `m=xy`. Every edge in their common
line-graph neighborhood joins `{u,v}` to `{x,y}`. The neighborhood has four
members, and there are only four possible simple cross-edges, so all of

```text
ux, uy, vx, vy
```

belong to `R`. At edge `uv`, however, splitness requires the two other edges
at `u` to be line-graph-antocomplete to the two other edges at `v`; `ux` and
`vx` share `x`. This contradiction eliminates all size-three points.

## The all-size-two branch

Every active point set now has size two. Let `F` be the spanning cubic graph
of consumed `K`-edges and put

```text
U = K - E(F).
```

Then `U` is a spanning 2-factor. At any active triangle `i`, its three
`F`-neighbors are pairwise `K`-adjacent by the local `222` rule. None of the
three pair edges can lie in `F`, since that would make three size-two point
sets pairwise meet at three distinct active triangles. Hence all three lie in
`U`, and `N_F(i)` is a triangle component of `U`. Every vertex belongs to
some `F`-neighborhood, so

```text
U = 4 C3.                                                     (8)
```

If one vertex of a `U`-triangle `A` has `F`-neighborhood equal to another
`U`-triangle `B`, symmetry and cubicity force all nine edges between `A` and
`B`. Thus the four triangles pair off and

```text
F = 2 K3,3,
K = 2 K6.
```

This contradicts (6), completing the equality exclusion.

## Failed shortcut and repair record

An earlier draft claimed that a single `223` occurrence of a size-three point
immediately forced a closed `K5` gadget. That was not justified: at another
occurrence, a fifth `K`-neighbor could in principle enter one of the size-two
point sets. The adversarial audit rejected that shortcut. The proof above does
not use it; the common-point rule instead shows that the possible old endpoints
cannot be reused, leaving only one endpoint where two are required.
